# Clear Goals and Immediate Feedback

Two characteristics of flow that work together: you know what to do next (clear goals) and you know how you're doing (immediate feedback). Without both, attention scatters and flow breaks.

---

## Clear Goals

Characteristic #7 of [[The Flow State]]: the activity has clear goals at every moment. The rock climber knows the next handhold. The chess player sees the next tactical threat. The surgeon knows the next step of the procedure.

Clear goals focus attention by eliminating ambiguity. When you don't know what to do next, the brain enters a decision-making mode that is incompatible with flow. Uncertainty produces anxiety; clarity produces focus.

---

## Immediate Feedback

Characteristic #8: the activity provides immediate, unambiguous feedback. The climber knows instantly whether the hold is secure. The musician hears whether the note is right. The writer sees whether the sentence works.

Immediate feedback closes the loop between action and result. It tells you whether you're moving toward the goal or away from it. Without feedback, you cannot adjust — and adjustment is what keeps the challenge-skill balance in the flow channel.

---

## Why Both Are Necessary

Clear goals without feedback: you know what to do but not whether it's working. This produces anxiety.
Feedback without clear goals: you know how you're doing but not where you're going. This produces confusion.

Together, they create a tight action-result loop that keeps attention focused and the flow state stable.

---

## Application

- **Work**: Break projects into clear milestones with measurable outcomes
- **Learning**: Practice problems with immediate answers (not passive reading)
- **[[The Pomodoro Technique]]**: Each pomodoro has a clear goal ("finish this section") and feedback ("did I finish?")
- **[[Deep Work]]**: Newport recommends shutting down rituals that include reviewing what was accomplished — building in feedback
