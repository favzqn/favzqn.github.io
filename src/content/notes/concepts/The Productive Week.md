# The Productive Week

The week is the forgotten unit of productivity. We plan days and years, but the week — with its natural rhythm of work and rest — is the sweet spot for sustained output.

---

## The Jumu'ah Reset

In [[The Productive Muslim]], Mohammed Faris structures the week around Jumu'ah (Friday) — the Islamic day of congregational prayer. Jumu'ah serves as a weekly reset: a pause for reflection, gratitude, and recalibration.

The week begins with Saturday (or Sunday, depending on the cultural context) as a building day, peaks midweek, and culminates in Jumu'ah as a spiritual checkpoint. This mirrors the Sabbath principle found across religious traditions — the idea that productivity requires periodic stopping.

---

## The Structure

- **Building days** (early week): High-energy tasks, deep work, new projects
- **Peak days** (midweek): Meetings, collaboration, execution
- **Wind-down days** (late week): Review, reflection, preparation
- **Reset day** (Jumu'ah/Sabbath): Prayer, family, rest — no work

This is not a rigid schedule but a rhythm. The point is to honor the natural ebb and flow of energy across the week, rather than treating every day as identical.
