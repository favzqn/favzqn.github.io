# The Good News Is...

A reframing technique: prefix any piece of information — even bad news — with "The good news is..." to shift the listener's attention toward the positive angle. It is the simplest and most powerful verbal frame in Phil Jones's *Exactly What to Say*.

---

## How It Works

The phrase "The good news is..." acts as a cognitive anchor. Before the listener can process the full message, their brain has already been primed to look for the positive. This is not deception — it is selective emphasis. Every situation has multiple angles. This phrase chooses the constructive one.

**Examples:**
- "The project is delayed. **The good news is**, we have time to get the details right."
- "You didn't get the job. **The good news is**, the one you really want is still open."
- "The price went up. **The good news is**, I can lock it in for you today."

---

## The Connection to Positive Labeling

This technique is a specific application of [[Positive Labeling]] — the broader principle that people live up to the labels you give them. When you frame a situation positively, you are labeling the listener as someone for whom good news exists. They rise to meet that label.

---

## Why It Works

- **Reframing** redirects attention from loss to opportunity
- **Priming** — the word "good" activates positive neural pathways before the full message arrives
- **Authority** — the speaker sounds confident and in control
- **Hope** — people will follow anyone who offers a path forward
