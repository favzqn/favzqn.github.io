# Eating as Ecological Act

"Eating is an agricultural act, as Wendell Berry famously said. It is also an ecological act, and a political act, too." — Michael Pollan, *The Omnivore's Dilemma*

---

## The Principle

Every meal is a vote. When you choose what to eat, you are choosing:
- What kind of farmer gets supported
- What kind of land management is practiced
- What kind of animal welfare is enforced
- What kind of food chain is sustained

The industrial food system is designed to make you forget this. Barcodes replace stories. Packaging hides origins. The distance between the eater and the eaten is maximized. [[Food Chain Transparency]] is the antidote.

---

## The Three Food Chains

Pollan traces three food chains in *The Omnivore's Dilemma*:
1. **Industrial**: Corn-based, petroleum-dependent, opaque. The chicken nugget.
2. **Organic**: A compromise between industrial and sustainable. Whole Foods.
3. **Foraging/Hunting**: Direct engagement with nature. The meal you gather yourself.

Each chain is a different ecological relationship. The industrial chain extracts from nature. The organic chain tries to work with nature (with varying success). The foraging chain participates in nature.

---

## The [[Wendell Berry]] Connection

Berry's original statement — "eating is an agricultural act" — was a call to consciousness. He argued that the modern eater has been severed from the agricultural reality of their food. We eat without knowing how the food was grown, who grew it, or what it cost the land.

Pollan extends this: eating is not just agricultural but ecological and political. Every calorie has a history — of sun, soil, water, labor, transport, processing. To eat consciously is to acknowledge that history.
