# Ask Questions Instead of Giving Orders

"Ask questions instead of giving direct orders." Questions preserve dignity, encourage cooperation, and make people feel like partners rather than subordinates.

---

## Key Ideas

- "Ask questions instead of giving direct orders"
- Questions give people ownership of the solution
- "People are more likely to accept an order if they have had a part in the decision that caused the order to be issued"
- Orders create resistance; questions create collaboration
- The best leaders guide through inquiry, not authority

---

## Why It Works

- Questions engage the other person's thinking — they become invested
- Orders trigger resistance — even if the order is reasonable
- Questions preserve the other person's sense of autonomy
- "Suggestions should be made in a way that they come from the other person's mind, not yours"
- People support what they help create

---

## How to Practice

- **Replace commands with questions** — "Could we try...?" instead of "Do this"
- **Ask for their input** — "What do you think about...?"
- **Use "how" and "what"** — these words invite collaboration
- **Give options, not ultimatums** — "Would you prefer A or B?"
- **Let them arrive at the conclusion** — patience is key

---

## The Leader's Paradox

- The most powerful person in the room asks the most questions
- Telling people what to do makes you feel powerful but makes them feel small
- Asking makes them feel valued and makes you look wise
- "The mark of a true leader is not how many followers they command, but how many leaders they create"
