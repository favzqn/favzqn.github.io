# Begin with Praise and Appreciation

"It is always easier to listen to unpleasant things after we have heard some praise of our good qualities." Start with what they did right before addressing what they did wrong.

---

## Key Ideas

- "Begin with praise and honest appreciation"
- "It is always easier to listen to unpleasant things after we have heard some praise of our good qualities"
- People are more receptive to criticism when they feel respected first
- Praise opens the ear; criticism closes it
- The sandwich method: appreciation → correction → encouragement

---

## Why It Works

- Praise lowers defenses and creates receptivity
- People who feel appreciated are more willing to accept feedback
- Starting with criticism triggers defensiveness — the message gets lost
- "A barber lathers a man before he shaves him" — preparation matters
- It preserves dignity while still addressing the issue

---

## How to Practice

- **Find something genuinely good** — every situation has something worth praising
- **Be specific** — "Your presentation was well-structured" vs "nice job"
- **Transition with empathy** — "I appreciate your effort, and I think we can make it even better"
- **End with encouragement** — "I know you can do this"
- **Don't fake it** — insincere praise undermines the entire message

---

## The Lincoln Example

- Lincoln wrote a letter to General Meade criticizing his failure to pursue Lee — but never sent it
- He understood that beginning with criticism would destroy the relationship
- His approach: acknowledge the good, then gently guide toward improvement
- "With malice toward none, with charity for all"
