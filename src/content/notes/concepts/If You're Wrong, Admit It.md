# If You're Wrong, Admit It

"If you are wrong, admit it quickly and emphatically." Humility disarms where defensiveness escalates. Admitting fault before being accused takes the wind out of criticism.

---

## Key Ideas

- "If you are wrong, admit it quickly and emphatically"
- "There is a certain degree of satisfaction in having the courage to admit one's errors"
- "I may be wrong. I frequently am. Let's examine the facts" — these words have magic
- Admitting error removes the other person's desire to attack
- Humility is not weakness — it is strategic strength

---

## Why It Works

- When you admit fault, the other person has nothing left to criticize
- It appeals to their sense of fairness — they become lenient
- It defuses anger before it builds
- "By fighting you never get enough, but by yielding you get more than you expected"
- People respect honesty more than they punish mistakes

---

## How to Practice

- **Admit it immediately** — don't wait to be caught
- **Be emphatic** — don't hedge or make excuses
- **Take full responsibility** — "I was wrong" not "mistakes were made"
- **Move forward** — after admitting, focus on the solution
- **Be specific** — acknowledge exactly what you did wrong

---

## The Paradox

- Admitting you're wrong makes people think you're usually right
- Humility builds credibility; arrogance destroys it
- The person who never admits fault loses trust; the person who admits it freely gains it
