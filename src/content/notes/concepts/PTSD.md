# PTSD

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder — a condition in which the body and mind remain trapped in the past, re-experiencing a traumatic event as if it is happening now. Not a disorder of the past but a disorder of the present: the trauma has not been processed, so the brain keeps replaying it.

---

## The Mechanism

When a person experiences overwhelming threat, the brain's alarm system (amygdala) fires at full intensity. The rational brain (prefrontal cortex) goes offline. The hippocampus — which normally timestamps memories as "past" — fails to encode the experience properly.

The result: the traumatic memory is stored as a present-tense sensory experience, not a past-tense narrative. A sound, smell, or visual trigger can reactivate the full trauma response — the body reacts as if the danger is happening now.

---

## The Three Clusters

1. **Re-experiencing**: Flashbacks, nightmares, intrusive memories. The trauma replays involuntarily.
2. **Avoidance**: Numbing, withdrawal, emotional flatness. The person avoids anything that might trigger the memory.
3. **Hyperarousal**: Insomnia, irritability, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response. The body stays in permanent fight-or-flight.

---

## Complex PTSD

Judith Herman identified **complex PTSD** — a variant caused by prolonged, repeated trauma (childhood abuse, domestic violence, captivity). In addition to the three standard clusters, complex PTSD includes:
- Disturbances in self-organization
- Affect dysregulation (emotional storms)
- Negative self-concept ("I am damaged")
- Disturbances in relationships

The [[Window of Tolerance]] narrows dramatically in complex PTSD — minor triggers produce major dysregulation.

---

## Treatment

- [[EMDR]]: Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing — helps the brain reprocess traumatic memories
- [[Polyvagal Theory]]: Working with the vagus nerve to regulate the autonomic nervous system
- Somatic experiencing: Body-based therapy that discharges stored survival energy
- [[Yoga for Trauma Recovery]]: Yoga widens the window of tolerance through body awareness
- Narrative therapy: Helping the person construct a coherent story from fragmented traumatic memories
