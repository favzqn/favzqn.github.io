# Make the Other Person Feel Important

"The deepest principle in human nature is the craving to be appreciated." Make people feel significant — and do it sincerely.

---

## Key Ideas

- "The deepest principle in human nature is the craving to be appreciated"
- "Always make the other person feel important"
- Everyone wants to feel significant — appeal to that need
- "If you are happy, you will be happy by making the other person feel important"
- The desire to be important is the deepest hunger in human nature

---

## How Carnegie Saw It

- William James: "The deepest principle in human nature is the craving to be appreciated"
- People will do almost anything for recognition
- Making someone feel important is the greatest gift you can give
- But it must be sincere — hollow flattery backfires
- "Everybody likes a compliment" — Abraham Lincoln

---

## How to Practice

- **Listen attentively** — nothing says "you matter" like undivided attention
- **Remember their name** — identity is the foundation of importance
- **Ask for their opinion** — it signals you value their mind
- **Acknowledge their contributions** — don't let effort go unnoticed
- **Be specific** — "Your insight on the project saved us" beats "good work"

---

## The Sincerity Test

- Would you say it to their face if they couldn't help you?
- Is it true and specific, or vague and flattering?
- Are you saying it because you mean it, or because you want something?
- "Flattery is telling the other person precisely what he thinks about himself"
