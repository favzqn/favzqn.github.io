# Benjamin Graham's Mr. Market

An allegorical figure invented by Benjamin Graham in *The Intelligent Investor* (1949). Mr. Market is your business partner who shows up every day offering to buy your share of a business or sell you his share — at wildly varying prices.

---

## The Allegory

Imagine you own a share of a private business worth $1,000. Your partner, Mr. Market, shows up every day and offers a price. Some days he's euphoric and offers $1,500. Other days he's depressed and offers $500. You are free to accept or ignore his offers.

The lesson: **the market is there to serve you, not to guide you.** Mr. Market's price tells you what he thinks — not what the business is worth.

---

## The Psychology

Mr. Market is manic-depressive. He swings between irrational exuberance and unjustified panic. Graham's genius was recognizing that most investors don't have a framework for valuing a business independently, so they take Mr. Market's word for it. This is the opposite of [[Deep Work]]-style independent thinking.

Warren Buffett calls Mr. Market "a drunk, bipolar idiot" and says the key to investing is recognizing when Mr. Market is sober and when he is not.
