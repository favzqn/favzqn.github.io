# Henry David Thoreau

American essayist, poet, and philosopher (1817–1862). Best known for *Walden; or, Life in the Woods* (1854), an account of the two years he spent living in a cabin he built near Walden Pond. The foundational text of American simplicity.

---

## The Walden Experiment

In 1845, Thoreau built a small cabin on land owned by Ralph Waldo Emerson. He lived there for two years, two months, and two days. His goal: to "live deliberately, to front only the essential facts of life."

> "I went to the woods because I wished to live deliberately, to front only the essential facts of life, and see if I could not learn what it had to teach, and not, when I came to die, discover that I had not lived."

---

## Influence on Digital Minimalism

Cal Newport uses Thoreau as the philosophical ancestor of the digital minimalism movement. Thoreau's argument — that most of what we call "necessity" is actually distraction from what matters — applies directly to the smartphone age.

Newport frames Thoreau's experiment as the original "digital declutter": a deliberate withdrawal from the noise of society to reconnect with what is essential. The cabin at Walden Pond is the 19th-century equivalent of turning off notifications.

---

## The Economy of Attention

Thoreau calculated the true cost of things not in dollars but in hours of life. A worker earning a dollar a day might spend half a day's labor on a luxury. The true cost is time — and time is attention. This is the same insight that drives [[The Attention Economy]] critique: our attention is being spent on things we don't value.
