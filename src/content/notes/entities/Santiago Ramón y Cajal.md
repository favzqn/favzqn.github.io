# Santiago Ramón y Cajal

Spanish neuroscientist (1852–1934). Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (1906). Father of modern neuroscience. His drawings of neural structures remain some of the most beautiful scientific illustrations ever produced.

---

## The Perseverance Principle

Cajal's most enduring idea is not a discovery but a statement: **"Perseverance is the virtue of the less brilliant."** He argued that the people who achieve great things in science are not necessarily the smartest — they are the ones who refuse to give up.

This is Barbara Oakley's central teaching in [[A Mind for Numbers]]. Oakley calls Cajal her hero precisely because his story is one of transformation through effort, not innate genius.

---

## From Troublemaker to Genius

As a child, Cajal was a disciplinary problem. He was expelled from school. He was arrested for vandalism. His father — a surgeon — apprenticed him to a barber and then a shoemaker to "cure" his rebelliousness.

Cajal eventually channeled his obsessive energy into anatomy and microscopy. His hand-drawn illustrations of neurons — each one a work of art — revealed that the nervous system is made of individual cells (the neuron doctrine), not a continuous network. This insight won him the Nobel Prize.
