# Wendell Berry

American novelist, poet, environmental activist, cultural critic, and farmer. Born 1934 in Henry County, Kentucky. Has farmed the same land since 1965. Called "the prophet of rural America."

---

## Key Ideas

- **"Eating is an agricultural act"** — the most quoted line in food philosophy. Every meal connects you to a farmer, a field, a system. You cannot separate what you eat from how it was grown.
- **Agrarian populism** — Berry argues that industrial agriculture destroys not just the land but the culture built around it. The farm is not a factory; it is an ecosystem and a community.
- **The Fidelity of Place** — Berry refuses to leave his land. His writing comes from staying in one place long enough to understand it deeply.

---

## Influence on *The Omnivore's Dilemma*

Michael Pollan draws heavily on Berry's agrarian philosophy. Berry's essays — *The Gift of Good Land*, *What Are People For?*, *The Unsettling of America* — provide the intellectual foundation for Pollan's critique of industrial food. Where Pollan investigates, Berry proclaims.

---

## The Contrarian

Berry is suspicious of technology, progress, and efficiency. He writes on a typewriter. He argues that the industrial mind — which sees land as a resource to be exploited — is the root of both ecological and cultural destruction. This places him in direct opposition to the [[Deep Work]]-style productivity frameworks that dominate modern thinking.
