# Paul Ekman

American psychologist (born 1934). Pioneer in the study of emotions and their relation to facial expressions. His research demonstrated that certain facial expressions are universal — hardwired into every human being, regardless of culture.

---

## Key Contributions

- **Universal emotions**: Ekman identified seven basic emotions that produce recognizable facial expressions across all cultures — happiness, sadness, fear, anger, surprise, disgust, and contempt
- **Microexpressions**: Fleeting facial expressions (1/25 to 1/5 of a second) that reveal concealed emotions. They are involuntary and nearly impossible to fake
- **The Facial Action Coding System (FACS)**: A comprehensive system for classifying every possible facial movement. Trained observers can decode the full range of human emotion from facial muscle movements alone

---

## Influence on Lie Detection

Ekman's work forms the scientific foundation for modern lie detection techniques. His research showed that when people try to conceal emotions, the true emotion leaks through as a microexpression. This is the basis for [[Lie Detection (Cognitive Load)]] — cognitive load increases the likelihood of microexpression leakage.

Patrick King, author of *Read People Like a Book*, draws heavily on Ekman's microexpression research. King translates Ekman's academic findings into practical techniques for everyday interactions.

---

## The Debate

Ekman's claims about universal emotions and reliable lie detection remain controversial. Critics argue that context matters more than facial expressions, and that trained observers are not significantly better than chance at detecting lies in real-world conditions. Barrett's theory of constructed emotion directly challenges Ekman's basic emotion model.
