# George Naylor

Iowa corn farmer. A third-generation farmer on 320 acres near Churdan, Iowa. Michael Pollan spent a week on Naylor's farm to understand the reality of industrial corn production in America.

---

## The Reluctant Monoculturalist

Naylor grows corn and soybeans — the two crops that dominate the American Midwest. He doesn't like it. He knows the system is broken. But he has no choice. The federal farm subsidies, the commodity markets, the seed companies, the equipment costs — all of them push toward corn monoculture.

Naylor is the opposite of [[Joel Salatin]]: a farmer trapped in a system he can see through but cannot escape. Where Salatin built an alternative, Naylor documents the problem from inside.

---

## What Pollan Learned

Naylor taught Pollan the economics of corn:
- **Farm subsidies** make corn artificially cheap
- **The commodity system** rewards volume, not quality
- **The seed companies** (especially Monsanto/Bayer) control what farmers plant
- **The end result**: corn is not a crop — it is an industrial input. It goes into ethanol, high-fructose corn syrup, animal feed, and processed food. Very little of it is eaten as actual corn.

---

## The System's Prisoner

Naylor represents the American farmer as a figure of tragic irony. He grows food he would never eat, using methods he knows destroy the soil, subsidized by a government that claims to support him while actually supporting agribusiness. He is [[Industrial Corn Monoculture]] made flesh.
