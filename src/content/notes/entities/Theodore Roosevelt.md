# Theodore Roosevelt

26th President of the United States (1858–1919). Known for boundless energy, progressive reform, and an almost supernatural ability to connect with people. Carnegie uses him repeatedly as the model of a leader who made everyone feel valued.

---

## The Charisma Principle

Roosevelt's secret was simple: he made every person feel like the most important person in the room. He would prepare for each meeting by reading up on his guest's particular interest — whether it was cattle ranching, ornithology, or naval strategy. He entered every conversation on the other person's ground.

A servant said of Roosevelt: "When he went hunting, he would sit up the night before reading up on the particular animal he was going after." This is [[Talk in Terms of the Other Person's Interests]] in action.

---

## Carnegie's Use of Roosevelt

Carnegie uses Roosevelt as the prime example of [[Make the Other Person Feel Important]]. Roosevelt didn't just remember names — he remembered what people cared about. He wrote personal letters. He followed up. He made people feel seen.

This is the opposite of the self-promoting leader. Roosevelt led by making others feel like leaders.
