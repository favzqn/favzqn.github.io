# Osamu Dazai

Japanese author (1909–1948). Born Tsushima Shūji into a wealthy family in northern Honshu. One of the most important writers of the postwar period. His work explores alienation, self-destruction, and the impossibility of sincerity.

---

## Life

Dazai's life reads like a rehearsal for his fiction. He attempted suicide multiple times — once with a barmaid, once with a university student, and finally successfully in 1948 by drowning in the Tamagawa Canal. He was 38.

He struggled with morphine addiction, was expelled from university, and was disowned by his family. He married three times. His second wife, Michiko, bore him children and tried to stabilize his life. He repaid her by leaving.

---

## *No Longer Human*

His masterpiece, *No Longer Human* (人間失格, 1948), is narrated by Yozo — a man who has been "disqualified from being human." The novel is semi-autobiographical. Dazai drowned before it was published.

The novel's three notebooks trace Yozo's life from childhood terror of human beings through adult dissolution — alcoholism, morphine addiction, and final institutionalization. It is the purest expression of alienation in Japanese literature.

---

## The Dazai Paradox

Dazai wrote about sincerity with extraordinary sincerity. He wrote about the impossibility of connection while producing work that connects deeply with millions of readers. The clown who describes clowning is still a clown — but he is also the only honest person in the room.

- [[The Clown]]: Dazai and Yozo both use humor as a survival mechanism against the terror of human judgment
- [[Fear of Human Beings]]: The core of Dazai's work — the conviction that other people are fundamentally unknowable
- [[Crime and Punishment]]: Dostoevsky's Raskolnikov and Dazai's Yozo are mirror images — one acts, the other withdraws

- [[The Body Keeps the Score]]: Dazai's self-destruction can be read as trauma expressed through the body — morphine, alcohol, suicide attempts
