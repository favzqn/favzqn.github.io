# Philosophy

Meaning, desire, the inner life, and the examined existence. This domain is not academic philosophy but lived philosophy — ideas that change how you act, not just how you think.

---

## The Books

- [[Tao Te Ching]] — Lao Tzu's 81 verses on effortless action ([[Wu Wei]]), simplicity ([[The Uncarved Block]]), and the paradox of power. The deepest book in the garden.
- [[Letters to a Young Poet]] — Rilke's advice: [[Living the Questions]], going into yourself, and [[Patience and Slowness]] as creative principles.
- [[The Almanack of Naval Ravikant]] — Naval's synthesis of Eastern philosophy, Western economics, and practical wisdom. [[Desire Is Suffering]], [[Happiness Is Learned]], and the pursuit of specific knowledge.
- [[Sapiens]] — Harari's grand narrative: [[Imagined Orders]], the agricultural revolution as history's biggest fraud, and the power of shared myths.

---

## Key Concepts

- [[Wu Wei]] — action through non-action; the Taoist ideal of effortless effort
- [[The Uncarved Block]] — simplicity and original nature; returning to what you were before the world told you what to be
- [[Desire Is Suffering]] — fewer desires = more peace; the Buddhist-Naval convergence
- [[Happiness Is Learned]] — the default state when nothing is missing
- [[Living the Questions]] — patience with uncertainty; not needing answers immediately
- [[Imagined Orders]] — shared myths (money, nations, religions) enable cooperation at scale
- [[Everything Passes]] — the exhausted truth from No Longer Human

---

## The Thread

The philosophical books in this garden trace a single thread: **Solitude → Presence → Effortless Action → Flow → Deep Work.** Lao Tzu provides the ancient framework. Rilke provides the creative application. Naval provides the modern synthesis. Harari provides the historical context.
