# Spirituality

Faith, meditation, and the inner life. This domain explores how spiritual practice — across traditions — produces the same results that psychology calls "flow" and neuroscience calls "alpha states."

---

## The Books

- [[The Productive Muslim]] — Mohammed Faris's framework for integrating Islamic spirituality with modern productivity. [[Productivity and Barakah]] — productivity infused with divine blessing. [[The Amanah Framework]] — time, body, and mind as trusts from Allah.
- [[Tao Te Ching]] — Lao Tzu's ancient text on [[Wu Wei]] (effortless action) and [[The Uncarved Block]] (simplicity). The philosophical backbone of the garden.
- [[The Silva Mind Control Method]] — Jose Silva's technique for entering [[Alpha Meditation]] states. Practical meditation without religious framework.
- [[Letters to a Young Poet]] — Rilke's spiritual advice: [[Living the Questions]], going into yourself, and [[Patience and Slowness]] as creative and spiritual disciplines.

---

## Key Concepts

- [[Wu Wei]] — action through non-action; the Taoist ideal
- [[Productivity and Barakah]] — productivity infused with divine blessing
- [[Alpha Meditation]] — entering alpha brainwave states for healing, creativity, and problem-solving
- [[The Amanah Framework]] — trusts from Allah: time, body, and mind
- [[Fasting and Productivity]] — Ramadan as a productivity training ground
- [[The Uncarved Block]] — simplicity and original nature
- [[Living the Questions]] — patience with uncertainty as a spiritual practice

---

## The Convergence

The spiritual traditions in this garden converge on a single insight: **the deepest productivity comes from surrender, not effort.** The Taoist surrenders to the Tao. The Muslim surrenders to Allah. Silva's practitioner surrenders to the alpha state. Rilke's poet surrenders to the questions.

This is the same insight that [[Flow]] describes in psychological terms: the best performance comes when the self-conscious ego gets out of the way.
