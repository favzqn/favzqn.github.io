# Propinquity and Mere Exposure

Friendship and trust increase linearly with simple interaction and exposure. The more we see someone, the more we like them.

---

## Propinquity Effect

Students in a dormitory were most likely to be friends with people on the same floor, especially those near mailboxes and staircases — places with the most traffic. Proximity creates friendship.

## Mere Exposure Effect

**Zajonc (1968)**: We prefer what's familiar. The more we see something, the more we like it. This applies to people, brands, music, and even nonsense words.

## How It Works

1. Show up consistently
2. Be visible — near high-traffic areas
3. Repetition creates familiarity
4. Familiarity creates trust
5. Trust creates likability

## In Practice

- The barista you see daily becomes someone you "know"
- The neighbor you wave to becomes someone you "trust"
- The colleague you eat lunch with becomes a friend
- Salespeople use this: multiple touchpoints before a sale

## Key Insight

> "Repetition creates trust. The more you see someone, the more likely you will become friends with them and comfortable with them."
