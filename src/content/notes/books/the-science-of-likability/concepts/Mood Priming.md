# Mood Priming

Trigger someone's positive memories by invoking the context, environment, and sensory details associated with those memories. They'll unconsciously slip into the mood they were in during that memory.

---

## The Research

**Eich, Macauley & Ryan (1994)**: Memories don't exist in a vacuum. They're linked to context, environment, events, and mood. Invoke the elements present during a positive memory and the mood follows.

**Byrne & Clore (1970)**: If people are nearby when we feel good, we begin to associate THEM with good feelings (Pavlovian conditioning). You become the bell that makes people smile.

## How It Works

1. Know someone's happiest memory
2. Identify the sensory details (sounds, smells, places, objects)
3. Bring up those details in conversation
4. They unconsciously slip into that positive mood
5. They associate YOU with that mood

## Example

Your friend Dorothy's best memory is her wedding — a garden with balloons, pillows, and classical music. Mention gardens, balloons, or classical music. She won't know why, but she'll feel good around you.

## Key Insight

> "Like Pavlov's dog, this isn't a process that we are fully aware of until we reach the end result of suddenly salivating."
