# Pygmalion Effect

People become who you expect them to be. Your expectations shape their behavior.

---

## The Research

**Rosenthal & Jacobson (1968)**: Students randomly labeled "gifted" (with no actual basis) performed significantly better. Teachers gave them preferential treatment, more attention, and more encouragement — and the students rose to meet the expectation.

## The Mechanism

1. You expect someone to be interesting → you engage them deeply → they show you their interesting side
2. You expect someone to be boring → you disengage → they never get the chance to show you otherwise
3. Your expectations create the reality you experience

## How to Use It

- Expect the best from people
- Treat them as if they're already the person you want them to be
- Give people the benefit of the doubt
- Your positive expectations create positive interactions

## The Danger

> "You create the world you reside in through your expectations. If you were told someone was boring before meeting them, you would approach with low expectations, ask surface-level questions, and receive the boring answers you expected."

## Key Insight

> "Never underestimate the power of your own expectations. You create the world you reside in through your expectations."
