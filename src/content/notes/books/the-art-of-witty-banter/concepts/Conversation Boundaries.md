# Conversation Boundaries

Stating at the outset of a conversation that it must be short, which paradoxically lowers the other person's guard and often extends the conversation.

---

## The Psychology

People are often on guard during conversations. They may not open up because they feel busy, tired, or think talking to you will take forever. An open-ended interaction with no perceived time limit is a huge mountain.

## The Technique

Make a "boundary statement" right at the beginning:

- "I only have a minute, but..."
- "I'm on my way out, but quick question..."
- "I know you're busy, so I'll be brief..."

## Why It Works

When someone knows the conversation has limits, they're more likely to say "Sure, why not" because they can imagine it will be brief. Once they realize the conversation is actually interesting and engaging, they'll open up and forget about the time limit.

## Key Insight

You're not putting a boundary on conversation topics, but on the conversation's length and the other person's investment in it. This makes you look respectful of their time.
