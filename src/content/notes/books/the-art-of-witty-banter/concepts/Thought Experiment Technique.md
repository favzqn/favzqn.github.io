# Thought Experiment Technique

Labeling hypotheticals as "thought experiments" to free people from the consequences of their answers and engage their problem-solving faculties.

---

## The Problem

Some people can't give a straight answer. They deflect, hedge, or refuse to engage with interesting questions because of fear of judgment or being wrong.

## The Technique

1. Announce: "Let's do a thought experiment"
2. Set ground rules: "This is purely hypothetical"
3. List objections and dismiss them: "Assume there's no legal consequence," "Assume your religion permits it"
4. Pose the question

## Why It Works

- Disassociates their answer from their identity
- Removes inhibitions and objections about honesty
- Says "You're free to say whatever you want; we're just exploring"
- Engages their problem-solving and intellectual faculties

## Example

"Thought experiment: your religion believes in reincarnation and no waste, so you don't feel you're disrespecting your potential meal. You're stranded in the mountains with no food. A companion has died. Would you be able to commit cannibalism if necessary?"

## Key Insight

The longer your list of dismissed objections, the more willing they'll be to answer. It's like saying "Be completely honest because there is no blowback."
