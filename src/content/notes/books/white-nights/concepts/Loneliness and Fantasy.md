# Loneliness and Fantasy

The feedback loop: loneliness drives fantasy, fantasy deepens loneliness.

---

## The Cycle

1. **Isolation** — the dreamer has no friends, no relationships
2. **Fantasy** — to cope, he builds an elaborate inner world
3. **Dependency** — the fantasy world becomes more real than reality
4. **Inability** — he cannot function in real situations
5. **Deepening isolation** — his inability drives people away
6. **Return to fantasy** — the cycle repeats

## Dostoevsky's Insight

> "I was terrified of being left alone, and for three whole days I wandered about the city in a state of deep melancholy."

The dreamer's loneliness is not chosen. It is the result of being unable to connect — and the inability to connect is the result of having spent too long in fantasy.

## The Breaking Point

Nastenka breaks the cycle — temporarily. For four nights, the dreamer lives in reality. He walks with a real person, feels real emotions, makes real plans. But when she leaves, the cycle threatens to resume.

## Key Insight

> "Feeling is not shattered, but becomes concentrated."

Loneliness doesn't destroy feeling — it intensifies it. The dreamer feels MORE, not less, because he is alone. This is both his curse and his gift.
