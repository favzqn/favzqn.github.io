# The Dreamer

Dostoevsky's portrait of a person who lives entirely in fantasy — rich in imagination, impoverished in reality.

---

## The Definition

> "A dreamer — if you require a precise definition — is not a man, but some sort of sexless being."

The dreamer lives in an inaccessible corner, hiding from daylight. He has a "whole world of his own" — palaces, adventures, heroism, love affairs that exist only in the mind.

## The Symptoms

- Cannot make conversation with real guests
- Retreats into fantasy when reality intrudes
- Has no friends, no relationships, no life
- Is happy when walking alone in nature, miserable when facing other people
- Knows he is wasting his best years but cannot stop

## The Paradox

> "You sense that this inexhaustible fantasy is finally growing tired, that it is becoming exhausted under constant strain; because, you see, you are growing into manhood, you are outgrowing your former ideals."

The dreamer's rich inner life is what makes him unable to live in the outer world. Fantasy is both escape and prison. The same imagination that creates beautiful worlds prevents him from inhabiting the real one.

## The Prognosis

> "More years will pass, followed by gloomy solitude, and then doddering old age will come on a walking-stick."

The dreamer sees his future clearly: loneliness, aging, the fading of fantasy, and nothing to replace it. He cannot stop it because he cannot change what he is.
