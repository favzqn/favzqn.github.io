# A Minute of Bliss

The novella's final line — the dreamer's answer to loss.

---

## The Moment

The dreamer loses Nastenka to the lodger. She writes a letter asking for forgiveness. He reads it, weeps, and then:

> "My God! A whole minute of bliss! Is that really so little for the whole of a man's life?"

## The Meaning

This is not defeat. It is acceptance. The dreamer has spent his life in fantasy, never experiencing real connection. Nastenka gave him four nights of genuine human contact — the only real connection he has ever known. He lost her, but he gained something: the knowledge that he is capable of real feeling.

## The Question

Is one minute of genuine happiness enough to justify a lifetime of loneliness? Dostoevsky's answer — through the dreamer — is yes. Not because loneliness is good, but because the capacity for bliss is what makes us human.

## Key Insight

The dreamer's tragedy is not that he lost Nastenka. It is that he will return to his fantasy world, carrying this one minute of real happiness, knowing the difference between fantasy and reality — and being unable to bridge it.
