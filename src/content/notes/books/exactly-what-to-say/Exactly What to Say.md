# Exactly What to Say

*Exactly What to Say: The Magic Words for Influence and Impact* by Phil M. Jones (2017).

---

## Summary

A practical handbook of 23 "magic words" — specific phrases and conversational frameworks that influence decisions. Jones argues that the difference between getting what you want and not getting it often comes down to a few carefully chosen words.

### Foundation: Two Principles

Before the magic words, Jones establishes two psychological foundations:

1. **Loss Aversion**: "People will work far harder to avoid a potential loss than they will to achieve a potential gain." People make decisions for emotive reasons first, then rationalize with logic.

2. **Feel Before Logic**: "Something has to feel right before it ever makes sense." If you're winning the argument based on logic alone, you're appealing to the wrong set of reasons.

### The 23 Magic Words

1. **"I'm going to give you..."** — Framing a commitment as a gift, not a ask
2. **"How would you feel if...?"** — Conditional future-facing scenario (loss aversion + visualization)
3. **"Just imagine..."** — Creates mental pictures; the brain cannot help but picture the scenario
4. **"When would be a good time to...?"** — Assumes yes; removes "I don't have time" objection
5. **"I'm guessing you haven't got around to..."** — Saves face; disarms excuses
6. **"Simple Swaps"** — Change open questions to closed: "What questions do you have for me?" / "What's the best number to contact you at?"
7. **"You have three options"** — Rule of three; stack choices in your favor; finish with "What's going to be easier for you?"
8. **"There are two types of people"** — Binary framing; prompt instant self-identification
9. **"I bet you're a bit like me"** — Pre-frames agreement; makes later disagreement harder
10. **"If... then"** — Conditional statements from childhood hardwiring: "If you give this a try, then I promise you won't be disappointed"
11. **"Don't worry"** — Instant anxiety relief; creates calm authority
12. **"Most people"** — Social proof: "What most people do is..." triggers herd behavior
13. **"The good news is..."** — Labeling technique; forces positive reframe
14. **"What happens next is..."** — Leads the conversation to close without asking permission
15. **"What makes you say that?"** — Regains control after an objection; forces explanation
16. **"Before you make your mind up..."** — Moves from "no" to "maybe"; keeps negotiation alive
17. **"If I can..., then will you?"** — Isolates the objection and removes the barrier
18. **"Enough"** — Quantity influence: "Would three be enough for you?" swings decision to the larger option
19. **"Just one more thing"** — The Columbo Method; downsell when guard is down
20. **"Could you do me a small favor?"** — Preloaded commitment; almost always gets yes
21. **"You wouldn't happen to know..."** — Referral request with built-in compliment
22. **"Just out of curiosity..."** — Preface for direct questions without sounding obnoxious
23. **"It works great" / "Good ones"** — Simple positive answers that stun people into agreement

### Core Philosophy

Jones's approach is not manipulation but **decision catalyst** — helping people make decisions they already want to make but are stuck on. His role definition: "professional mind-maker-upper."

---

## Key Themes

1. **Words Create Worlds** — small changes in language produce large changes in outcomes
2. **Emotion Before Logic** — feelings drive decisions; logic justifies them
3. **Loss Over Gain** — avoiding loss is more motivating than achieving gain
4. **Control Through Questions** — the person asking questions controls the conversation
5. **Social Proof** — "most people" and "two types of people" leverage herd behavior
6. **Face-Saving** — techniques that let people save face while moving toward your goal
7. **The Columbo Method** — downsell after apparent failure
