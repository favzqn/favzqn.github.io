# The Direct Question

"What makes you say that?" — the universal objection handler.

---

## How It Works

When someone raises an objection, they seize control of the conversation. "What makes you say that?" shifts control back to you by forcing them to explain themselves.

The objection is treated as a question, not a statement. The person must now justify their position rather than simply stating it.

---

## The Silence

After asking, **be quiet**. Jones warns: "Silence becomes your friend." The person will either:
1. Give a real, honest answer (12 seconds of silence)
2. Realize they don't have a real objection ("You know what, you're right")

Either way, you've moved the conversation forward.

---

## The Application

- "I haven't got the time." → "What makes you say that?"
- "I need to shop around." → "What makes you say that?"
- "I haven't got the money." → "What makes you say that?"

The technique works because most objections are not real reasons — they're social scripts. When forced to explain, people often discover their objection is hollow.
