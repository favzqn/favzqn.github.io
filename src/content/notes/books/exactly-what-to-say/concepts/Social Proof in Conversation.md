# Social Proof in Conversation

"Most people" — two words, ten letters, responsible for more negotiating success than any other single strategy.

---

## How It Works

Jones exploits two psychological realities:

1. **Safety in numbers**: People take confidence from knowing that people like them have already made the decision
2. **Permission to follow**: Sometimes people need to be told what to do, but without their permission it sounds rude

When you say "most people do X," the listener's subconscious says: "I'm most people, so if that's what most people do, then perhaps that's what I should do too."

---

## The Application

- "What most people do is complete the forms with me here today"
- "Most people in your circumstances would grab this opportunity with both hands"
- "There are two types of people in this world: those who... and those who..."

The "two types" version is even more powerful — it forces instant self-identification.
