# Lie Detection (Cognitive Load)

Most people — even professional lie detectors — are barely better than chance at detecting lies. The cognitive load technique increases the mental burden on the liar until they make mistakes.

---

## The Problem

- A 2006 study found people were generally bad at detecting liars regardless of age, education, or confidence
- Some estimates: only 50 in 20,000 people can spot a liar more than 80% of the time
- Even FBI/CIA agents are only slightly better than chance

---

## The Techniques

### 1. Establish a Baseline
Know how the person behaves when telling the truth. Ask about neutral topics first.

### 2. Increase Cognitive Load
Lying requires extra mental effort — maintaining the story, monitoring your body, reading the listener. Increase this burden:
- Ask unexpected questions
- Add details they must incorporate
- State something untrue yourself and watch their response
- Ask them to relay a true story you already know, then compare to the suspected lie

### 3. Watch for Incongruence
When words and body don't match, the body is usually telling the truth. Look for clusters:
- Verbal denial + physical approach (leaning in while saying "I'm not interested")
- rehearsed timing (pauses too perfect, answers too smooth)
- Emotional mismatches (saying "I'm happy" with a microexpression of sadness)

### 4. Watch for Timing Mismatches
Liars often respond too quickly ( rehearsed) or too slowly (constructing the lie). Truth-tellers have natural pauses.
