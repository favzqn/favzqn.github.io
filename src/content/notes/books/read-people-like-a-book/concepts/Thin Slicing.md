# Thin Slicing

Using small amounts of data to make accurate assessments. Snap decisions based on thin slicing can be surprisingly accurate — but must be supplemented with deliberate analysis.

---

## The Principle

Your unconscious mind processes vastly more data than your conscious mind. Thin slicing leverages this by trusting initial gut reactions, then verifying them with deliberate observation.

Judges use it ("court sense"), military and police officers use it, firefighters use it. People use it to find romantic partners.

---

## The Caveat

Thin slicing can be distorted by:
- **Confirmation bias** — looking for evidence to prove your snap judgment
- **Projection** — assuming others think like you
- **Cultural differences** — gestures mean different things in different contexts
- **Personality** — an introvert's silence is not the same as a hostile person's silence

---

## The Practice

1. Notice your knee-jerk reaction to someone
2. Allow it to guide you to deeper analysis
3. Challenge your first impression — is it real or projection?
4. Don't brush off your instinctive response, even if you can't explain it
