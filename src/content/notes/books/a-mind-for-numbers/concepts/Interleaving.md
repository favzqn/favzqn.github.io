# Interleaving

"Varying your learning by hopping back after a break to strengthen an approach you've learned earlier."

---

## The Principle

Instead of studying one topic exhaustively before moving to the next (blocked practice), interleave — mix different topics or problem types in a single study session.

Example: Instead of doing 20 of the same type of calculus problem, do 5 of type A, 5 of type B, 5 of type C, then 5 mixed.

---

## Why It Works

Interleaving forces your brain to:
- **Discriminate** between problem types
- **Choose** the right strategy for each problem
- **Build flexible knowledge** that transfers to new situations

Blocked practice feels easier but produces weaker, less durable learning. Interleaving feels harder but produces stronger, more flexible knowledge.

---

## Evidence

Karpicke's research shows that retrieval practice with interleaving produces dramatically better learning outcomes than blocked practice or simple rereading.
