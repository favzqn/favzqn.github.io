# Illusion of Competence

"When we read material over and over, the material becomes familiar and fluent, meaning it is easy for our minds to process. We then think that this easy processing is a sign that we have learned something well, even though we have not."

---

## The Problem

Most students use the strategy of repeated reading — simply reading through notes over and over. This "passive and shallow strategy often produces minimal or no learning." It's called "labor in vain."

The illusion of competence arises because:
- Familiarity feels like understanding
- Fluency in reading feels like mastery
- The brain processes familiar material easily, creating a false signal

---

## The Solution

- **Retrieval practice**: Close the book and try to recall what you just read
- **Self-testing**: Quiz yourself instead of rereading
- **Feynman technique**: Explain the concept in simple terms
- **Practice problems**: Work problems without looking at solutions first
