# Reverse Dieting

A controlled increase in calories post-diet to recover metabolic rate while minimizing fat gain.

---

## The Problem

After a diet, the body's "restore" prong is active: hunger is elevated, metabolic rate is suppressed, and fat storage is enhanced. If you jump straight back to pre-diet calories, you'll regain fat rapidly — potentially MORE than you lost (body fat overshooting).

## The Solution

Increase calories in a controlled manner over time:
- Add 5-15% to carbs and fats weekly
- Monitor weight — if it increases by more than 0.5%, pause
- Continue until calories reach predicted maintenance (or above)
- The body's metabolic rate gradually recovers

## Why It Works

Reverse dieting gives calories a chance to go in concert with the return of metabolic rate. Jumping calories up quickly creates a gap between energy intake and the body's ability to process it — the excess is stored as fat.

## High Flux Principle

Research shows that eating more + exercising more (high flux) prevents fat cell hyperplasia, even with the same relative energy gap. The body processes more energy without storing it.

## Key Insight

> "The purpose of reverse dieting is NOT to lose weight. It's to recover your metabolic rate so that when you diet again, you're starting from a better position."
