# Flexible Dieting

Track your macros (protein, carbs, fats) and eat whatever you want within those targets. No "good" or "bad" foods.

---

## The Principle

- If it fits your macros, you can eat it
- No food is off-limits
- No "clean" vs. "dirty" food distinction
- A calorie is a calorie regardless of source

## Why It Works

**Rigid dieting** → all-or-nothing thinking → one "bad" food → "I've already ruined it" → binge → guilt → repeat

**Flexible dieting** → moderation → one cookie fits your macros → no guilt → continued adherence

Research shows: rigid dieting correlates with eating disorders, higher BMI, and more weight regain. Flexible dieting does not.

## How to Track

1. Weigh your food (kitchen scale)
2. Log in an app (MyFitnessPal, etc.)
3. Hit your protein, carb, and fat targets daily
4. Allow flexibility in food choices

## The Budget Metaphor

Your macros are a budget. A Snickers bar costs 256 calories. If your budget is 2000 calories, that's 12.8% — doable but expensive. As your calories drop during a diet, "expensive" foods become harder to fit.

## Key Insight

> "The best diet is the one you can stick to. Flexible dieting works because it removes the psychological burden of restriction."
