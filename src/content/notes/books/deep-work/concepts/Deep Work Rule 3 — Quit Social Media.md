# Rule #3: Quit Social Media

Adopt a craftsman approach to tool selection — don't use tools just because they're popular.

---

## The Craftsman Approach

Ask of any tool: "Does this tool support my deep work values, or does it actively harm them?"

If a tool doesn't significantly advance your core goals, remove it.

---

## Key Arguments

### The Any-Benefit Mindset (Don't Do This)
- "Twitter helps me network" → but does it?
- "Facebook keeps me connected" → but at what cost?
- "I might miss something" → FOMO drives shallow work

### The Craftsman Approach (Do This)
- Identify your core goals
- Evaluate each tool against those goals
- Only keep tools that provide significant, positive impact

---

## Key Example: Baratunde Thurston

- "Most connected man in the world" — 59,000 Gmail conversations, 1,500 Facebook posts/year
- Disconnected for 25 days
- Result: less stress, more presence, conversations with strangers, bought a bike
- "The end came too soon" — but he went back to it

---

## Two Rules for Social Media

1. **Keep your core leisure activities.** Don't quit everything — just the stuff that doesn't add value.
2. **Use the craftsman approach.** Don't assume you need a tool; prove you do.
