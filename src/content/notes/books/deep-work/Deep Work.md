## Key Definitions

> **Deep Work:** Professional activities performed in a state of distraction-free concentration that push your cognitive capabilities to their limit. These efforts create new value, improve your skill, and are hard to replicate.

> **Shallow Work:** Noncognitively demanding, logistical-style tasks, often performed while distracted. These efforts tend to not create much new value in the world and are easy to replicate.

> **The Deep Work Hypothesis:** The ability to perform deep work is becoming increasingly rare at exactly the same time it is becoming increasingly valuable in our economy. As a consequence, the few who cultivate this skill, and then make it the core of their working life, will thrive.

---

## Core Argument

1. **Ch1 — Valuable:** Deep work produces rare and valuable skills (Nate Silver, David Heinemeier Hansson, John Doerr)
2. **Ch2 — Rare:** Open offices, social media, instant messaging all push toward shallowness
3. **Ch3 — Meaningful:** Deep work produces flow, satisfaction, and a life well-lived (Ric Furrer, Duke cannon building)

## Practical Rules

1. **Rule 1 — Work Deeply:** Choose a depth philosophy (monastic/bimodal/rhythmic/journalistic), build rituals, execute like a business
2. **Rule 2 — Embrace Boredom:** Train concentration, meditate productively, practice deep scheduling
3. **Rule 3 — Quit Social Media:** Don't use tools just because they're popular; adopt craftsman approach
4. **Rule 4 — Drain the Shallows:** Fixed-schedule productivity, minimize shallow work, make every minute count
