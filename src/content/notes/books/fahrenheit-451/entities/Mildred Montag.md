# Mildred Montag

Guy Montag's wife. She has chosen the parlour walls over her husband, her thoughts, and her life. She is the novel's portrait of voluntary numbness.

---

## Characteristics

- Overdoses on sleeping pills and doesn't remember
- Calls the parlour wall characters her "family"
- Can't remember where she met Montag
- Reports Montag's hidden books to the authorities
- Drives 100 mph to feel something
- Is "happy" — and proud of it

## The Choice

Mildred is not a victim. She is not controlled. She has chosen the walls. When Montag reads poetry to her friends and Mrs. Phelps cries, Mildred is furious — not because the poetry is dangerous, but because it made her feel something.

## The Mirror

In the moment before the bombs hit, Mildred sees her own face reflected in the parlour wall — a mirror instead of a screen. For one instant, she sees herself.

## Key Insight

Mildred is Bradbury's portrait of the person who has everything and nothing. She has entertainment, comfort, stimulation — and she is empty. She is the argument for why books matter: not because they provide information, but because they provide meaning.
