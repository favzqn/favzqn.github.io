# Granger

The leader of the intellectual outcasts — men who have memorized books and carry them in their heads. He is the novel's voice for memory as resistance.

---

## His Philosophy

> "We're nothing more than dust jackets. The books are on file behind our eyes, the books are waiting, with their pages uncut, for the customer who may never come."

Granger's group doesn't fight fire with fire. They fight fire with memory. Knowledge survives by being carried in human minds.

## The Phoenix

Granger tells the Phoenix myth: the bird that burns and is reborn. But humans have one advantage: memory. We can remember what we did wrong and choose differently.

## His Grandfather

Granger tells a story about his grandfather — a carpenter who said: "It doesn't matter what you do, so long as you change something from the way it was before you touched it into something that's like you after you take your hands away."

## Key Insight

Granger represents the long view. The city is destroyed, but what they carry is not. They will rebuild — and this time, they will remember.
