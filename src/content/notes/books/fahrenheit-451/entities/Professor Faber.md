# Professor Faber

A retired English professor who taught literature and lost his courage. He is Montag's guide and the novel's voice for what books actually contain.

---

## His Failure

Faber knew what was happening — the simplification, the burning, the end of books — and did nothing. He stayed in his house, afraid, for years. He is the intellectual who saw the danger and chose safety over action.

## What Books Contain

> "Books show the pores in the face of life. The comfortable people want only wax moon faces, poreless, hairless, expressionless."

Faber defines three things books provide:
1. **Detail** — the texture of life, not the summary
2. **Leisure** — time to digest what is read
3. **The right to carry out actions** — based on what was learned from 1 and 2

## His Courage

Through the green bullet, Faber guides Montag during the disastrous evening with Mildred's friends. He trembles, he panics, but he stays connected. For the first time in years, he is doing something.

## His Departure

After Montag kills Beatty, Faber flees to the countryside to join the book-memorizers. He has finally found the courage he lost decades ago.

## Key Insight

Faber is the novel's portrait of the intellectual who knows the right thing but is too afraid to do it. His arc is the arc of cowardice redeemed — not by strength, but by necessity.
