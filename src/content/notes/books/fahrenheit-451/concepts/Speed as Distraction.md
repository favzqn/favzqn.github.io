# Speed as Distraction

Everything in Bradbury's world moves fast enough to prevent thought. Cars, billboards, entertainment — all designed to keep the mind from settling.

---

## Examples

- **Cars**: Montag's uncle was jailed for driving 40 mph. The minimum is 55. Cars move so fast you can't read billboards — so billboards stretch to 200 feet.
- **Billboards**: expanded from 20 to 200 feet because cars rush by too quickly to read shorter ones.
- **Entertainment**: classics cut to 15-minute shows, then 2-minute columns, then dictionary entries. Everything boils down to "the gag, the snap ending."
- **Football, fun parks, races**: constant stimulation that requires no thought.

## Clarisse's Observation

> "People don't talk about anything. They name a lot of cars or clothes or swimming-pools mostly and say how swell! But they all say the same things and nobody says anything different from anyone else."

## Bradbury's Prediction

The acceleration of media — from books to radio to TV to social media to TikTok — follows exactly the trajectory Bradbury described. Content gets shorter, faster, more sensational. The "snap ending" is the algorithm.

## Key Insight

Speed is not a side effect of the system. It IS the system. A mind that is always moving cannot settle on a thought. A person who is always stimulated cannot notice what is missing.
