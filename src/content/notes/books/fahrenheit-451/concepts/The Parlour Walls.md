# The Parlour Walls

Room-sized television screens that serve as Mildred's real family. She interacts with the characters on the walls more than with her husband. The "family" in the walls is more real to her than the person beside her in bed.

---

## The Technology

- Three walls are installed; the fourth is the goal
- Interactive scripts — Mildred reads her lines from a prompter
- The characters address her by name
- Cost: $2,000 per wall
- The "relatives" — Montag's term for the gibbering, meaningless presences

## The Effect

- Mildred can't remember where she met Montag
- She overdoses on pills without noticing or caring
- She reports Montag's books to the authorities
- She chooses the walls over her husband, her thoughts, her life

## Bradbury's Prediction

Written in 1953, decades before flat screens, streaming, social media, and smartphones. The parlour walls are interactive television that demands participation — a prediction of reality TV, social media feeds, and algorithmic content that addresses you by name.

## Key Insight

> "If the parlour is my third wall, and my fourth wall is the real world, then I'm living in a box."

The parlour walls don't just distract — they replace. They are the substitute for front porches, conversation, thought, and human connection.
