# Big Organic vs Little Organic

"Everything eventually morphs into the way the world is."

---

## The Tension

When organic farming went mainstream, it split into two camps:

**Big Organic**: General Mills (Cascadian Farm), Earthbound Farm ($350 million organic salad company). Industrial-scale organic that compromises original principles — long-distance shipping, confinement dairy, synthetic inputs allowed under USDA rules.

**Little Organic**: Small farms like Polyface, community-supported agriculture (CSAs), farmers' markets. Focus on local, seasonal, pasture-raised, beyond-organic practices.

---

## The Compromise

The USDA's National Organic Program (2002) created rules that favored industrial organic:
- Organic dairy cows can be confined and grain-fed
- Hydroponic growing counts as organic
- Synthetics are allowed if the Organic Trade Association lobbies hard enough
- The word "organic" was captured by the largest players

---

## The Result

Small organic farmers can't compete on price with industrial organic. But they compete on quality, transparency, and ecological integrity.
