# Causal Fallacies

Errors in attributing cause and effect. The most common fallacies in everyday reasoning.

---

## Post Hoc Ergo Propter Hoc

"After this, therefore because of this." The rooster crows, then the sun rises. Therefore the rooster caused the sunrise. Sequence does not equal causation.

> "I wore my lucky socks and we won. Therefore the socks caused the win."

## Cum Hoc Ergo Propter Hoc

"With this, therefore because of this." Correlation as causation. Ice cream sales and drowning rates both rise in summer. Therefore ice cream causes drowning. (Both are caused by heat.)

## Ex-Post-Facto Statistics

Using statistics after the event to explain what happened. "There was a one-in-a-million chance of that happening!" But something had to happen — the probability of SOME outcome was 100%.

## Non-Anticipation

Failing to consider that circumstances might change. "This policy has always worked." (But conditions are different now.)

## Key Insight

> "The human mind is wired to see patterns and assign causes. This is useful for survival but dangerous for reasoning. The causal fallacies exploit our need to explain everything."
