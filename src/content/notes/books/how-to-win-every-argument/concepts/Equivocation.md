# Equivocation

Shift the meaning of a word mid-argument. The same word is used in two different senses, but treated as if it means the same thing.

---

## How It Works

1. Use a word in one sense for the premise
2. Shift to a different sense for the conclusion
3. The audience doesn't notice the shift

## Example

> "The sign said 'fine for parking here,' so I parked." (Fine as penalty vs. fine as acceptable.)

> "We should all be concerned about the RIGHT to bear arms. Everyone has the RIGHT to bare arms in summer." (Right as entitlement vs. right as appropriate.)

## Why It Works

English (and all natural languages) are full of words with multiple meanings. The audience processes the sentence as coherent because the word LOOKS the same.

## How to Use It

> "When a word can have two meanings, use the one which suits your argument, and slide seamlessly to the other when it suits your conclusion."

## How to Defend

Ask: "Which meaning of that word are you using?" Pin down the definition before the argument proceeds.
