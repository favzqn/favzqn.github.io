# Slippery Slope

Exaggerate the consequences of an action to make it seem more dangerous than it is. "If we allow X, then Y will happen, then Z, and then catastrophe."

---

## How It Works

1. Start with a reasonable first step
2. Claim it will lead to an extreme outcome
3. Provide no evidence for the chain of causation
4. Conclude the first step must be prevented

## Example

> "If we allow same-sex marriage, next they'll want to marry animals."

> "If we regulate guns at all, next they'll confiscate every weapon."

## Why It Works

People fear consequences more than they evaluate evidence. The emotional weight of the extreme outcome overwhelms the rational assessment of the first step.

## The Runaway Train Variant

Take the opponent's argument and extend it to absurdity. "If you think we should help the homeless, why not house everyone for free? Why not give everyone a million dollars?"

## How to Defend

Ask: "What evidence do you have that X will lead to Y?" Most slippery slopes have no evidence for the chain.
