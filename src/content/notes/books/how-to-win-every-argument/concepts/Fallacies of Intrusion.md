# Fallacies of Intrusion

Introducing what doesn't belong. Material irrelevant to the argument is brought in to sway the audience.

---

## Key Fallacies

### Blinding with Science
Use technical jargon to overwhelm. "The epistemological framework of the hermeneutic circle precludes..." (Sounds impressive, says nothing.)

### Emotional Appeals
Six types: ad metum (fear), ad odium (hatred), ad superbiam (pride), ad modum (fashion), ad superstitionem (superstition), ad invidiam (envy).

### Loaded Words
Use emotionally charged language instead of neutral terms. "The regime MURDERED protesters" vs "The regime USED FORCE against rioters." Same event, different framing.

### Poisoning the Well
Discredit before they speak. "He's funded by the oil industry." Now nothing they say matters.

### Irrelevant Humour
Make a joke to deflect from the argument. "Ha ha, good point! Anyway..." The audience laughs, the argument is forgotten.

### The Slippery Slope
Exaggerate consequences. "If we allow this, next they'll want THAT, and then..." Without evidence for the chain.

### Wishful Thinking
Accept something because you WANT it to be true. "The business will succeed. You'll get a huge return!" (Based on desire, not evidence.)

## Key Insight

> "Fallacies of intrusion work because they tap into emotions that bypass rational thought. Detection requires asking: 'Is this actually relevant to the argument?'"
