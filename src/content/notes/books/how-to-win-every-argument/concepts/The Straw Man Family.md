# The Straw Man Family

Misrepresenting an argument to make it easier to attack. The most common fallacy in political and online discourse.

---

## The Core: Straw Man

Misrepresent your opponent's position, defeat the misrepresentation, then claim to have defeated their actual position.

> "My opponent wants to let criminals roam free!" (They actually want sentencing reform.)

## Variants

### Refuting the Example
Attack the weakest example of their argument rather than the argument itself. If they cite ten studies, find the one with the smallest sample size and demolish it.

### Poisoning the Well
Discredit the person BEFORE they speak. "Of course he'd say that — he's funded by the oil industry." Now nothing they say carries weight.

### Red Herring
Introduce an irrelevant topic to divert attention. "Why worry about climate change when there are children starving?"

### The Runaway Train
Exaggerate the opponent's position to absurdity. "If we allow any regulation of guns, next they'll be confiscating all weapons and we'll be defenseless."

### Shifting Ground
Change your argument when the original is defeated, pretending you held the new position all along.

## Key Insight

> "The straw man is the most common fallacy because it's the easiest to deploy. You don't need to understand the opponent's argument — you just need to misrepresent it."
