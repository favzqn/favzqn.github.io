# Ad Hominem

Attack the person, not the argument. The most common fallacy in political discourse.

---

## Two Forms

### Abusive
Direct attack on the character of the arguer. "You can't trust his opinion on education — he didn't even finish college." (His argument's validity doesn't depend on his education.)

### Circumstantial
Attack the arguer's situation or interests. "Of course he supports the oil company — he owns shares." (His financial interest doesn't make his argument wrong.)

## Tu Quoque (You Too)
"You did it too!" — deflect criticism by pointing to the accuser's record. Doesn't address whether the criticism is valid.

## Why It Works

People conflate the source of an argument with the argument itself. A bad person can make a good argument. A good person can make a bad argument. The argument's validity is independent of who makes it.

## How to Use It

> "When you cannot fault the argument, attack the arguer. The audience will often confuse the two."

## How to Defend

Name it: "That's an ad hominem. Can you address the actual argument?"
