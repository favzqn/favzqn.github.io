# Trauma

Trauma is not the event itself, but the imprint that experience leaves on the mind, brain, and body. It fundamentally changes how a person experiences themselves and the world.

---

## Definition

Trauma occurs when a person is overwhelmed by an experience that exceeds their capacity to cope. The brain's alarm system (amygdala) goes into overdrive, while the rational brain (prefrontal cortex) goes offline. The body remains in survival mode long after the danger has passed.

> "Trauma is not what happens to you. Trauma is what happens inside you as a result of what happened to you." — Gabor Maté

---

## Key Ideas from *The Body Keeps the Score*

1. **Trauma lives in the body** — it is not just a psychological event but a physiological one
2. **Fragmentation** — trauma shatters the integrated sense of self
3. **Triggering** — the body re-experiences trauma as if it is happening now
4. **Imprinting** — traumatic memories are stored differently from normal memories
5. **Repetition compulsion** — survivors unconsciously recreate traumatic situations

---

## How Trauma Changes the Brain

- **Amygdala** becomes hyperactive (constant threat detection)
- **Prefrontal cortex** goes offline (can't think clearly)
- **Hippocampus** shrinks (can't distinguish past from present)
- **Default mode network** is disrupted (loss of sense of self)

---

## Types of Trauma

- **Acute trauma** — single overwhelming event
- **Chronic trauma** — repeated, prolonged exposure
- **Developmental trauma** — childhood abuse, neglect, abandonment
- **Complex trauma** — multiple traumatic events, often interpersonal
- **Vicarious trauma** — witnessing others' trauma (secondary trauma)
