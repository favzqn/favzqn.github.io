# The Power of Disclosure

Telling your story — in therapy, in writing, or to a trusted person — is a critical step in trauma recovery. Secrets keep trauma alive; disclosure transforms it.

---

## Why Disclosure Matters

- Secrets require constant energy to maintain
- Suppressed memories and feelings remain "locked" in the body
- Telling the story externalizes it — it becomes something that happened, not something that defines you
- Being heard and believed is inherently healing

---

## How Disclosure Heals

- It organizes the chaotic experience into a narrative
- It allows the brain to integrate the memory into normal memory
- It activates Broca's area (speech center), which goes offline during trauma
- It creates connection with the listener, which activates the social engagement system

---

## Forms of Disclosure

- **Talk therapy** — telling the story to a therapist
- **Writing** — journaling, memoir, poetry
- **Art** — expressing the inexpressible through creative work
- **Group therapy** — sharing with others who understand
- **Testimony** — telling your story publicly (court, advocacy)

---

## Important Caveats

- Disclosure must happen in a safe environment
- Telling the story too fast can retraumatize
- The goal is integration, not re-experiencing
- Safety and regulation must come first
