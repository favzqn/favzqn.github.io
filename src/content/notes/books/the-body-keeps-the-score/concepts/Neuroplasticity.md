# Neuroplasticity

Neuroplasticity is the brain's capacity to reorganize itself by forming new neural connections. It is the biological basis for hope in trauma recovery.

---

## Definition

The ability of the brain to change and adapt throughout life in response to experience, learning, and injury. Old neural pathways can weaken; new ones can form.

---

## Why It Matters for Trauma

- Trauma rewires the brain toward survival (fear, hypervigilance)
- These pathways become automatic and habitual
- Neuroplasticity means these patterns are not permanent
- New experiences, therapies, and practices can create new pathways

---

## How Neuroplasticity Works in Recovery

- **Repeated new experiences** strengthen new neural pathways
- **Therapy** creates corrective emotional experiences
- **Body-based practices** (yoga, movement) rewire body-brain connections
- **Neurofeedback** directly trains brainwave patterns
- **Safety and connection** activate the social engagement system

---

## Limitations

- Neuroplasticity works best with consistent, repeated practice
- It is slower in adults than in children
- Trauma in childhood has deeper imprints because the brain is still developing
- But recovery is always possible at any age
