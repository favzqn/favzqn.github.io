# Neurofeedback for Trauma

Neurofeedback trains the brain to regulate its own electrical activity. Van der Kolk's research showed it could help traumatized people achieve calmer, more focused brain states.

---

## How It Works

- Sensors on the scalp measure brainwave activity in real time
- A computer displays the brain's electrical patterns (as a video game or sound)
- The brain learns to shift toward calmer patterns through feedback
- Over time, the new patterns become automatic

---

## Why It Works for Trauma

- Trauma creates habitual patterns of brainwave dysregulation
- Neurofeedback directly retrains these patterns at the brain level
- It does not require talking about the trauma
- It can help when other therapies have stalled

---

## Types Used in Trauma

- **Alpha-theta training** — promotes calm, meditative states
- **SMR training** — improves focus and reduces hyperarousal
- **Z-score training** — compares brain activity to normative database

---

## Limitations

- Requires specialized equipment and trained practitioners
- Benefits may fade without continued practice
- Not a standalone treatment — best combined with other approaches
- Still relatively new; more research needed
