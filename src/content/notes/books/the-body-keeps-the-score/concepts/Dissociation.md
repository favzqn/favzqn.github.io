# Dissociation

Dissociation is the mind's way of escaping unbearable experience. When fight or flight is impossible, the brain disconnects from the present moment to survive.

---

## Definition

A disconnection between a person's thoughts, feelings, memories, and sense of identity. It exists on a spectrum from mild (daydreaming) to severe (dissociative identity disorder).

---

## How It Works in Trauma

- The brain "shuts down" awareness of the body and surroundings
- The person may feel detached, numb, or like they're watching a movie of themselves
- It is an automatic survival response, not a choice
- It becomes habitual when trauma occurs in childhood, before the brain fully develops

---

## Forms of Dissociation

- **Depersonalization** — feeling detached from yourself, like you're outside your body
- **Derealization** — the world feels unreal, dreamlike, or distant
- **Dissociative amnesia** — inability to recall important personal information
- **Structural dissociation** — the personality splits into "apparently normal" and "emotional" parts

---

## Why It Matters

- Dissociation prevents processing of traumatic memories
- It makes it difficult to feel safe in the body
- It interferes with relationships and daily functioning
- Recovery requires gradually reconnecting with the body and present moment
