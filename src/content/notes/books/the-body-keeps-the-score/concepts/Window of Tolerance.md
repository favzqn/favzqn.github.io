# Window of Tolerance

The window of tolerance is the zone of optimal arousal where a person can function, think clearly, and regulate emotions. Trauma shrinks this window dramatically.

---

## Definition

Coined by Dan Siegel, the window of tolerance describes the zone of arousal in which a person can manage emotions, think clearly, and handle daily stress. Outside this window, the person shifts into hyperarousal (panic, rage) or hypoarousal (shutdown, numbness).

---

## The Two Extremes

**Hyperarousal** (above the window):
- Anxiety, panic, rage
- Hypervigilance, insomnia
- Emotional flooding
- Fight-or-flight response

**Hypoarousal** (below the window):
- Numbness, shutdown
- Dissociation, collapse
- Emotional flatness
- Freeze response

---

## Trauma Shrinks the Window

- People with trauma have a narrower window of tolerance
- Minor triggers can push them into hyper- or hypoarousal
- The goal of recovery is to widen the window gradually
- This is achieved through body-based and relational therapies

---

## How to Widen the Window

- **Grounding techniques** — reconnect with the present moment
- **Breathwork** — regulate the autonomic nervous system
- **Movement** — discharge stored survival energy
- **Safe relationships** — co-regulation with trusted others
- **Gradual exposure** — slowly expand capacity to handle distress
