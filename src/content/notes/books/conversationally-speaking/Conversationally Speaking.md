# Conversationally Speaking

*Conversationally Speaking: WHAT to Say, WHEN to Say It, and HOW to Never Run Out of Things to Say* by Patrick King (2015).

---

## Summary

A comprehensive guide to conversation skills covering every phase from icebreaking to exiting gracefully, organized into 19 chapters plus a 21-day bootcamp plan. King's approach treats conversation as a science of pattern recognition — learnable, practicable, and systematic.

### Ch 1: Master Conversation; Relationships

Success requires strong relationships. Relationships are built through conversation that allows people to connect deeply. Small talk is the gateway — a filtering mechanism for trust, not a meaningless ritual.

### Ch 2: Everyone Likes a Verbal Mirror

The best conversationalist is the person who talks the least. Become a verbal mirror — shine the conversation back onto the other person. People love to talk about themselves. Your job is to guide, not to perform. Key phrases: "Tell me more about ____." "How did that affect you?"

### Ch 3: Icebreaking

Start with what you have in common at that moment. The environment, the host, the event — there's always a shared reality. Then narrow into the specific: their background, their interests, their story. Lead the conversation to them after breaking the ice. Avoid controversy at this stage.

### Ch 4: Common Questions, Uncommon Answers

The first ten questions are always the same. Prepare three interesting answers for each. This is King's "double explanations" technique from Art of Witty Banter, applied systematically.

### Ch 5: Effective Listening in Three Steps

1. Don't interrupt — fighting the urge to dominate
2. Give safe emotional space — don't talk over them
3. Reflect and guide — use their details to steer deeper

Listening is an exercise where you become closer by hearing someone's narrative and relating to it.

### Ch 6: Bulletproof First Impression

- Make eye contact — smile with your eyes
- Use an expressive voice — exaggerate positivity
- Focus on similarities — people open up to those who resemble them
- Reflect emotional cues — laugh when they laugh, be somber when they're somber
- Consistent body language — words, tone, and body must agree

### Ch 7: Storytelling

- Focus on the central point — every detail must relate to the thesis
- Ensure emotional payload — stories must make people feel something
- Paint a picture — let them smell what you smell, see what you see

### Ch 8: Safe Topics into Failsafe Conversations

Any topic can become a great conversation. It's not about the topic — it's about how you expand beyond the material and create a connection regardless. Focus on the other person's relationship to the topic.

### Ch 9: Avoid Awkward Silences

Mentally catalog details your conversation partner provides. Their hometown, occupation, hobbies — things that made their face light up. You always have something to return to.

### Ch 10: Bodily Speaking

Body language is vast, but in conversation: lean in = engaged, lean back = disinterested. Nodding = agreement. Crossed arms = closed off. Much of our damaging body language is subconscious.

### Ch 11: Social Cues

Social cues reduce ambiguity. They tell us what people are really saying. Key cues: nodding (agreement), mentioning a topic (want to discuss it), one-word answers (conversation is over), not turning to face you (they're done).

### Ch 12: Exiting Conversations with Grace

The recency effect — people remember the last thing best. Leave on a high note. Give them opportunities to finish stories. Deliver the feedback they seek. Use a witty summation as your exit line.

### Ch 13: Conversation Killers

Topics that kill: excessive complaining, controversial opinions shared too early, monopolizing the conversation, not reading social cues, oversharing.

### Ch 14: Introvert's Party

Introversion is not shyness. Introverts need solitude to recharge. The key is managing energy, not changing personality. Set time limits, arrive early (smaller crowd), and have exit strategies.

### Ch 15: Confronting Without Confrontation

- Praise-criticism-praise sandwich — start positive, insert critique, end positive
- Focus on consequences, not judgments — "I get muddy when you park there" vs "You park badly"
- Walk them through your analysis — let them reach the conclusion themselves
- Be solution-oriented — focus on actionable resolutions, not who's right

### Ch 16: Listen to Open Them Up

- Compliment the act of sharing — appreciate their vulnerability
- Create a safe space — no negative emotions, reveal your own vulnerabilities
- Determine the main message — what's the underlying motivation?
- Don't interrupt — let them finish their train

### Ch 17: Digitally Speaking

- Make it 100% crystal clear — without body language, ambiguity multiplies
- Smile on the phone — it changes your voice and mood
- Emote more than you think necessary — digital strips nuance

### Ch 18: 21-Day Bootcamp

A structured plan: week 1 (practice eye contact, smile, one conversation/day), week 2 (practice storytelling, mirroring, social cues), week 3 (practice confrontation, deep listening, digital skills).

### Ch 19: Humor 101

Humor is about timing, surprise, and self-deprecation. Don't force it. The best humor comes from observations about the shared situation.

## Key Themes

1. **Guide, Don't Perform** — your job is to steer, not to impress
2. **Verbal Mirror** — reflect conversation back to the other person
3. **Small Talk Is Real Talk** — it's a filtering mechanism for trust, not a waste of time
4. **Storytelling Is the Cornerstone** — conversation is a series of stories
5. **The Recency Effect** — how you leave matters more than how you arrive
6. **Pattern Recognition** — conversation is a learnable science, not an innate talent
7. **Safe Emotional Space** — people open up when they feel unjudged
