# Deep Listening

Hearing is not listening. Deep listening involves understanding motivations, creating safety, and getting people to open up like they would to a close friend.

---

## The Four Steps

### 1. Compliment the Act of Sharing

Appreciate that they took the time, effort, and courage to share. This sets the process off on the right foot. Without it, the situation feels confrontational.

### 2. Create a Safe Space

People aren't forthcoming because they don't feel safe from judgment. Three ways to create safety:
- Don't show negative emotions when they express opinions
- Reveal your own vulnerable aspects first
- Tell them explicitly that you don't judge

### 3. Determine the Main Message

People aren't always organized in their thoughts. There's a reason they're sharing — find the underlying motivation. Dig deeper into what matters most.

### 4. Don't Interrupt

Talking over someone is interpreted as threatening. Let them fully explain. You're not in a debate — you're there to earn their trust.

## Key Insight

> "Just because you are able to hear what somebody else is saying doesn't necessarily mean you are listening to them."
