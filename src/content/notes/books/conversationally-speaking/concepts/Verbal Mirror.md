# Verbal Mirror

Shine the conversation back onto the other person. Let them talk about themselves. Your job is to guide, not to perform.

---

## The Principle

People love to talk about themselves. The average person navigates the world with themselves as the center. Piggyback on this tendency so they do all the work.

## How It Works

1. Your job is NOT to come up with something profound, witty, or intelligent
2. Your job IS to guide the conversation and let the other person provide the content
3. Add small bits in from time to time to lure them deeper
4. The better you are at guiding, the safer you feel

## Key Phrases

- "Tell me more about ____."
- "Oh? How did that affect you?"
- "How did you come up with that?"
- [Repeat the last few words and trail off...]
- "Why did you think of that?"
- "What was the best or worst part of that?"
- "Why do you think that happened?"

## The Paradox

The best conversationalist is the person who talks the least. When you remove the spotlight from yourself, the performance anxiety disappears — and suddenly you can talk with anybody.

## Key Insight

Conversations suffer because people feel they are in the spotlight — performing, waiting to be judged. The verbal mirror removes that pressure entirely. The conversation is about them, and they feel the same comfort talking about something as familiar as themselves.
