# Icebreaking with Commonality

Start with what you have in common at that moment. Then narrow into the specific.

---

## The Method

1. Identify the shared reality — the environment, the host, the event
2. Open with a broad commonality: "How do you all know the host?"
3. Transition to the specific: their background, their story
4. Lead the conversation to them

## Sample Question Chain

1. "Did you see that cow head on the wall? This place is crazy!"
2. "I've only seen something like that in Texas. Have you been?"
3. "Oh, where were you born?"
4. "Why did you move here?"
5. "What do you do within [their field]?"
6. "Is that what you were looking for — work-life balance?"
7. "Where's your next vacation?"

From décor → origin → motivations → desires in seven questions.

## Rules

- You do 75% of the leading at the beginning
- Fill the silences yourself
- Avoid controversy — save opinions for when you've gauged compatibility
- Start broad, then narrow into the specific

## Key Insight

> "Start with a broad commonality, then narrow into the opposite. Focus into people's specific traits after breaking the ice with a shared reality."
