# Crime and Punishment

*Crime and Punishment* by Fyodor Dostoevsky (1866). Barnes & Noble Classics Series edition with introduction by Gary Rosenshield.

---

## Structure

- **Introduction** — by Gary Rosenshield, literary analysis
- **Part One** (Chapters 1-7): The murder and its immediate aftermath
- **Part Two** (Chapters 1-7): Raskolnikov's illness and first encounters with Porfiry
- **Part Three** (Chapters 1-6): The investigation deepens, Marmeladov's death
- **Part Four** (Chapters 1-6): Confrontation with Sonia, confession to Sonia
- **Part Five** (Chapters 1-5): Luzhin's plot, Svidrigailov's confession
- **Part Six** (Chapters 1-8): Final confrontation with Porfiry, confession
- **Epilogue** (Chapters 1-2): Siberia, Sonia follows, spiritual awakening

## Plot Summary

### Part One
Raskolnikov, a destitute former law student, plans and murders Alyona Ivanovna, a pawnbroker, and her half-sister Lizaveta. He steals a small purse and some trinkets but is unable to use them. He falls into illness and delirium.

### Parts Two-Three
Raskolnikov attempts to recover while avoiding detection. He meets Porfiry Petrovich, the examining magistrate, who begins to suspect him. Marmeladov is killed by a carriage; Raskolnikov gives his last money to the family.

### Parts Four-Five
Raskolnikov confesses his crime to Sonia, who reads him the story of Lazarus. Luzhin attempts to frame Sonia for theft; Raskolnikov exposes him. Svidrigailov reveals his own dark secrets.

### Part Six
Porfiry finally confronts Raskolnikov directly, revealing he knows the truth. Raskolnikov confesses and is sentenced to eight years in Siberia.

### Epilogue
In Siberia, Raskolnikov remains cold and unrepentant until Sonia follows him. He finally breaks down, weeps at her feet, and begins spiritual rebirth. The novel ends with the promise of a "new story."
