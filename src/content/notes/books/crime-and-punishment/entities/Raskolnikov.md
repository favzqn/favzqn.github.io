# Raskolnikov

Rodion Romanovich Raskolnikov. Protagonist of *Crime and Punishment*. A destitute former law student who commits murder to test his theory of extraordinary men.

---

## Key Traits

- Brilliant but impoverished
- Proud, isolated, prone to abstraction
- Oscillates between compassion and cold logic
- Suffers from fever and delirium after the murder
- Unable to use the stolen money or trinkets

---

## The Extraordinary Man Theory

Raskolnikov divides humanity into two categories:
1. **Ordinary people** — must live in obedience
2. **Extraordinary people** — have the right to transgress moral law if their ideas require it

He sees Napoleon as the model: a man who stepped over laws to achieve greatness. He murders Alyona to test whether he is "extraordinary."

---

## The Murder

- Kills Alyona Ivanovna (the pawnbroker) with an axe
- Accidentally kills Lizaveta (Alyona's gentle half-sister)
- Steals a purse and some trinkets
- Never opens the purse, forgets what the trinkets are
- The guilt immediately begins destroying him

---

## Psychological Torment

- Fever and delirium for days after
- Avoids everyone, fears detection
- Compulsive behavior: returns to the scene, talks too much
- oscillates between confession and self-justification
- His name "Raskolnikov" comes from "raskol" (schism/division)

---

## Redemption

- Confesses to Sonia, reads Lazarus story
- Confesses to Porfiry
- Sentenced to 8 years in Siberia
- Initially cold and unrepentant in prison
- Finally breaks down, weeps at Sonia's feet
- Begins spiritual rebirth — "the beginning of a new story"
