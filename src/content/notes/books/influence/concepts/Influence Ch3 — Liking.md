# Chapter 3: Liking — The Friendly Thief

Detailed chapter on the five weapons of liking and how they're exploited.

---

## Core Principle

We are more easily influenced by people we like — even people we've never met.

---

## The Five Weapons of Liking

### 1. Physical Attractiveness (Halo Effect)
- Attractive people assumed to be smart, kind, talented
- George Clooney endorsing evolution → people became more accepting
- Emma Watson → same result (not just male celebrity)
- Works regardless of age, sex, or religiosity

### 2. Similarity
- We like people who are similar to us
- Salespeople mirror body language, speech patterns
- Shared interests, backgrounds, affiliations
- Tupperware parties: friends selling to friends (10x more effective)

### 3. Compliments
- Even insincere compliments work
- We like people who say nice things about us
- Brain scans show compliments activate reward centers
- Salespeople use flattery to build rapport

### 4. Contact and Cooperation
- Mere exposure effect: familiarity breeds liking
- Contact must be cooperative, not competitive
- Shared goals create bonds
- University roommates who share rooms develop stronger friendships

### 5. Conditioning and Association
- We like things associated with good things
- Positive associations: weather, celebrities, beauty
- Negative associations: bad news, unpleasant people
- Companies associate products with attractive images and positive emotions

---

## The Tupperware Story

- Tupperware "home parties" are a classic compliance setting
- Sales 10x higher than store sales
- Works because of: Liking + Social Proof + Reciprocity + Commitment
- The friend selling is the key — not the product

---

## How It's Exploited

- Salespeople build rapport before selling
- Attractive spokespeople for products
- "Friend of a friend" marketing
- Conditioning: associating products with positivity
- Compliments as a tool of manipulation

---

## Defense

- Separate the liker from the deal
- Ask: "Would I buy this if the salesperson were different?"
- Recognize when rapport is manufactured
- Be skeptical of flattery before a sale
