# Social Proof

When people are free to do as they please, they usually imitate one another. We look to others to determine correct behavior, especially when uncertain.

---

## How It Works

- The greater the number of people who believe something, the more correct it seems
- We use others' behavior as a shortcut for our own decisions
- Especially powerful when we are uncertain or the situation is ambiguous

---

## Key Research

- **Beijing restaurant experiment:** Labeling dishes as "most popular" → sales jumped 13-20%
- **London pub experiment:** Sign stating "most popular beer" → porter sales doubled
- **Solomon Asch conformity experiments:** People conformed to obviously wrong answers when group agreed
- **Stanley Milgram obedience experiments:** People shocked others when told by authority figure

---

## How It's Exploited

- "Most popular" / "best seller" labels
- Testimonials and reviews
- Laugh tracks on sitcoms
- "Join 10 million satisfied customers"
- Social media likes and follower counts

---

## Social Proof Backfires

- **Planned Parenthood:** Donation requests with "your friends have donated" → donations decreased (people thought they already gave)
- **Suicide reporting:** Publicizing suicide methods increases similar suicides (copycat effect)

---

## Defense

- Check if the "proof" is genuine or manufactured
- Consider whether the group is knowledgeable about the decision
- Be aware that popularity ≠ quality
