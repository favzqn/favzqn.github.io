# Liking

We are more easily influenced by people we like. This applies even to people we've never met closely.

---

## The Five Weapons of Influence

1. **Physical attractiveness** — halo effect; attractive people are assumed to be smart, kind, talented
2. **Similarity** — we like people who are like us (dress, background, interests)
3. **Compliments** — we like people who say nice things about us, even when insincere
4. **Contact and cooperation** — familiarity breeds liking (mere exposure effect)
5. **Conditioning and association** — we like things associated with good things (weather, positivity)

---

## Key Research

- **Darwin's evolution acceptance:** Not increased by logic, but by liking the messenger
- **Tupperware parties:** Sales doubled when friends sold to friends (liking + social proof)
- **Joe Girard:** "Greatest car salesman" — sent birthday cards to 13,000+ customers

---

## How It's Exploited

- Salespeople build rapport before selling
- Compliments (even obvious ones) work
- Attractive spokespeople for products
- "Friend of a friend" marketing
- Conditioning: associating products with attractive images/celebrities

---

## Defense

- Separate the liker from the deal
- Ask: "Would I buy this if the salesperson were different?"
- Recognize when rapport is manufactured
