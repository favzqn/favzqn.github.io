# Instant Influence: Primitive Consent for an Automatic Age (Chapter 9)

How the seven principles are amplified in the digital age.

---

## The Problem

Digital technology has taken our natural shortcuts and turned them into weapons:
- Social media platforms are built on these principles
- Online shopping is designed to trigger automatic responses
- The speed of online interaction leaves no time for deliberation

---

## Principle Applications Online

| Principle | Online Application |
|-----------|-------------------|
| [[Reciprocation]] | Free content → email signup → sales funnel |
| [[Liking]] | Influencer marketing, parasocial relationships |
| [[Social Proof]] | Reviews, likes, follower counts, "X people bought this" |
| [[Authority]] | Expert badges, verified accounts, professional profiles |
| [[Scarcity]] | Flash sales, countdown timers, "only X left" |
| [[Commitment and Consistency]] | Free trials → automatic renewal, public posts |
| [[Unity]] | Online communities, "fellow members," group identity |

---

## The Joe Pyne / Frank Zappa Story

- Pyne: "Your long hair makes you a girl"
- Zappa: "Your wooden leg makes you a table"
- Illustrates how single features (long hair → girlishness) can trigger automatic judgments
- Digital age amplifies this with instant, superficial judgments

---

## Defense

- Slow down before clicking "buy" or "agree"
- Recognize the principles at work in digital environments
- Ask: "Would I make this decision if I had more time?"
