# Unity

The "we" is the shared me. We say yes more easily to people we consider "one of us." This is about identity, not just similarity.

---

## The Key Distinction

- **Liking:** "That person is like us" (similar)
- **Unity:** "That person is one of us" (same group/identity)

---

## How It Works

- We automatically divide people into "we" and "they"
- Inside our tribes, everything influence-related is easier
- Those within "we" get more agreement, trust, help, liking, cooperation
- Even infants show in-group favoritism

---

## The Concentric Circles of Unity

1. **Race/ethnicity** — strongest in-group bias
2. **Nationality** — "fellow countrymen"
3. **Religion** — shared faith = shared identity
4. **Age/generation** — "fellow millennials"
5. **Geography** — "fellow New Yorkers"
6. **Interests** — "fellow gamers," "fellow runners"
7. **Family** — "one of the family"

---

## Key Research

- **Nazi concentration camp guard:** Spare the 11th prisoner because he "looked like" the guard's son (unity principle saved a life)
- **Uniforms:** Shared uniforms instantly create unity (sports teams, military, organizations)

---

## How It's Exploited

- "Fellow [group] members" marketing
- Shared identity language ("we," "us," "our")
- Community building around brands
- "You're one of us" messaging

---

## Defense

- Ask: "Am I being influenced because of shared identity or genuine merit?"
- Consider whether the "we" framing is authentic or manufactured
- Recognize when group identity is being used to bypass critical thinking
