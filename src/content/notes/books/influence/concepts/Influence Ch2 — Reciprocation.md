# Chapter 2: Reciprocation — The Old Give and Take

Detailed chapter exploring the rule of reciprocation and how it shapes human interaction.

---

## Core Principle

We should try to repay what another person has provided us. This obligation is cross-cultural and deeply embedded in human society.

---

## Key Experiments & Stories

### Christmas Card Study
- Professor sent cards to perfect strangers
- Cards poured back from people who had never met him
- Mechanical, automatic response — "click, run"

### Charity with Sweets (UK)
- Investment bankers asked for a full day's salary donation
- When preceded by a small packet of sweets, **contributions more than doubled**
- Works even for requests of $1,000+

### Mexico–Ethiopia Relief ($5,000)
- In 1985, Mexico donated $5,000 in relief to Ethiopia during famine
- Decades earlier, Ethiopia had donated to Mexico during a flood
- The cycle of reciprocity spanned generations and continents

### Lincoln's Debt Repayment
- Abraham Lincoln, as a lawyer, once took a case for free
- Later discovered the opposing lawyer had once done HIM a favor
- Withdrew from the case, saying he couldn't fight against his benefactor

---

## How Compliance Professionals Exploit It

### The "But First" Technique
- Give something small BEFORE asking for the sale
- Free samples, gifts, favors → creates obligation
- Example: Waiters who give mints with the check get bigger tips

### Denial-Then-Retreat
- Start with a large request (knowing it will be refused)
- Retreat to a smaller, more reasonable request
- The smaller request seems like a concession → triggers reciprocity

### The Door-in-the-Face Technique
- Same as denial-then-retreat but framed as a concession
- "Okay, if you can't do $100, how about $25?"

---

## Defense

- Recognize when a "favor" is really a sales tactic
- Judge the original intent — was it genuine or strategic?
- Reciprocate the original favor, not the manipulation
- Separate the gift from the obligation
