# Chapter 7: Commitment and Consistency — Hobgoblins of the Mind

Detailed chapter on how past commitments lock us into future behavior.

---

## Core Principle

Once we make a choice or take a stand, we encounter personal and interpersonal pressures to behave consistently with that commitment.

---

## Key Experiments & Stories

### Amazon's "Pay to Quit"
- Employees offered $5,000 to leave every year
- Very few take the offer
- Those who stay become MORE committed
- Jeff Bezos: "Please Don't Take This Offer" — the decision to stay solidifies allegiance

### Sara and Tim (The Breakup Story)
- Sara broke up with Tim, got engaged to another man
- Tim begged her back, offered to quit drinking
- Sara chose Tim over the other boyfriend
- Tim never quit drinking, never married her
- Sara became MORE devoted after choosing him
- Key insight: the hard choice FORCED commitment

### The Foot-in-the-Door Technique
- Start with a small request
- Once agreed, escalate to larger requests
- Past commitment creates pressure to stay consistent
- Example: Asking to sign a petition → then donate → then host a meeting

### Lowball Pricing
- Offer a low price to get commitment
- After commitment, reveal hidden fees/costs
- Customer already committed → pays anyway
- Example: Car dealers, vacation packages

---

## The Key Mechanism

Small, initial commitments → bigger commitments over time

1. Write the story of your life (establish a position)
2. Feel pressure to stay consistent with that story
3. Consistency makes life simpler and easier
4. But it can also trap us in bad decisions

---

## Public vs. Private Commitments

- **Public commitments** are harder to break (social pressure)
- **Written commitments** are even stronger
- **Effort-based commitments** are most powerful (hazing, rituals)
- The more effort invested, the stronger the commitment

---

## How It's Exploited

- Free trial → automatic renewal → price increase
- Public declarations of goals (social media posts)
- "Just try it" → commitment to use → escalation
- Consistency leveraged by cults, political movements, sales

---

## Defense

- Ask: "Am I doing this because I want to, or because I said I would?"
- Be aware of escalation of commitment
- Consider whether your past commitment still serves you
- Don't let consistency override wisdom
