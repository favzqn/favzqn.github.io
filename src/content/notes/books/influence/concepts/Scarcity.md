# Scarcity

We want what is rare or disappearing. Opportunities seem more valuable when they are less available.

---

## How It Works

- Fewer things = more value (economically obvious)
- But also: losing something makes it seem more valuable than gaining it (loss aversion)
- Restrictions on freedom make us want the restricted thing more (psychological reactance)

---

## Two Key Triggers

1. **Scarcity of information** — "New!" (first-time availability)
2. **Scarcity of availability** — "Limited time!" / "Only 3 left!"

---

## Key Research

- **Divorce lawyer scenario:** "We have a deal. All you have to do is agree" → works because it frames the offer as something about to be lost
- **Forbidden fruit effect:** Adolescents told they can't read a book about sex → wanted to read it more
- **Auction mania:** Bidding wars escalate because each bid signals scarcity

---

## How It's Exploited

- "Limited time offer"
- "Only 2 left in stock"
- Flash sales
- Countdown timers
- "Exclusive" access
- "One-time only" pricing

---

## The "New!" Factor

- New information becomes more persuasive
- Old information (even if identical) loses persuasive power
- This explains why "new and improved" works on products

---

## Defense

- Ask: "Would I want this if it were readily available?"
- Separate genuine scarcity from manufactured urgency
- Pause before acting on "limited time" pressure
