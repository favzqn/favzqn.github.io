# Levers of Influence (Chapter 1)

How automatic triggers work in humans and animals.

---

## Fixed-Action Patterns

- Animals have built-in "click, run" behaviors triggered by specific stimuli
- Turkey: "cheep-cheep" sound → maternal instinct activates → embraces natural enemy
- Red-breast feather → Courtship dance in greylag geese
- These patterns are complex but triggered by single features

---

## Human Automaticity

- We have similar "click, run" patterns
- We use stereotypes and rules of thumb to classify things by a few key features
- Trigger features activate automatic responses
- This saves time and energy but can be exploited

---

## Key Examples

- **Turquoise jewelry story:** Employee marked "price x ½" as "price x 2" → sold out at double price
  - Expensive = good stereotype kicked in
  - Tourists with no knowledge of turquoise used price as quality trigger
- **Energy drink experiment:** Volunteers charged $0.89 solved FEWER puzzles than those charged $1.89
  - Cheap = less effective (even though the drink was the same)

---

## The Rule

We don't have time to analyze every situation. We use shortcuts:
- Expensive = good
- Famous = expert
- Popular = good
- Authority = trustworthy

These shortcuts usually work but can be exploited.
