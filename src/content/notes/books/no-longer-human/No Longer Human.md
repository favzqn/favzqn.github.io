# No Longer Human

*No Longer Human* (人間失格, *Ningen Shikkaku*, literally "Disqualified as a Human Being") by Osamu Dazai (1948). Translated by Donald Keene.

---

## Summary

The novel is structured as three notebooks discovered after the disappearance (and possible suicide) of their author, Yozo. A prologue and epilogue frame the notebooks through the eyes of an unnamed narrator who finds them "poignant and fascinating."

### Prologue
The narrator describes three photographs of Yozo — childhood, student days, and a final, monstrous image where the face has no expression, no individuality, and "could never be made the subject of a painting, not even of a cartoon." The narrator is repulsed and fascinated in equal measure.

### The First Notebook — Childhood
Yozo describes his life as "a life of much shame." Born in a wealthy family in rural Northeast Japan, he is surrounded by people he cannot understand. He invents "clowning" — a performance of absurdity and humor — to mask his terror of human beings. Key moments:

- **The train bridge**: He mistook a utilitarian bridge for an exotic decoration, and lost interest when he learned its purpose.
- **Mealtime dread**: He found the ritual of family meals inexplicable and threatening.
- **The lion mask incident**: When his father asks what present he wants, Yozo cannot answer. He sneaks into his father's notebook at night and writes "lion mask" to restore his good humor — a desperate performance even in private.
- **Servant abuse**: Maids and menservants commit "the ugliest, vilest, cruelest crime" against him as a child. He cannot tell anyone because he has no faith that human beings would listen.

### The Second Notebook — Youth
Yozo's clowning matures. At a seaside high school, he becomes popular through his antics. Then **Takeichi** — a puny, unremarkable boy — taps him on the back and whispers: *"You did it on purpose."*

This is Yozo's first great disaster. Takeichi has seen through the mask. Yozo tries to win him over, eventually inviting him home where Takeichi delivers a "demoniacal prophecy": *"I'll bet lots of women will fall for you."*

Yozo's world expands:
- He is drawn into a Communist underground movement — attracted not by its ideals but by its "irrationality," a world where "rational gentlemen" are absent.
- Women are drawn to him instinctively. He cannot understand them. He tolerates them out of passivity and fear.
- He meets **Tsuneko**, a café waitress, and spends one night of genuine happiness — the only night in his life he uses the word "happiness" without qualification. In the morning, he flees.

### The Love Suicide at Kamakura
At Tsuneko's suggestion, they attempt suicide together. She dies. He is saved. The incident is treated prominently in the press. His father disowns him. He is expelled from college. He is charged as an accomplice to suicide but released.

### The Third Notebook — Adulthood
Yozo spirals downward:
- He lives under **Flatfish**, a guarantor who speaks in riddles and never says plainly what he means.
- He becomes a second-rate cartoonist for cheap magazines.
- He marries **Yoshiko**, a woman of "divine ignorance."
- Yoshiko is violated by a clerk; Yozo watches in silence.
- He discovers morphine through a crippled pharmacy woman, becomes addicted, produces pornographic drawings to fund his habit.
- He writes to his father. No answer.
- Flatfish and Horiki find him and take him to a **mental hospital**.
- He is released. His father is dead. His brother buys him a house in the countryside.
- He lives with an old servant who occasionally violates him. He is 27 but looks over 40.

### The Final Line
> "Now I have neither happiness nor unhappiness. Everything passes."

### Epilogue
The narrator visits the café madam who gave him the notebooks. She says of Yozo: *"He was a good boy, an angel."* The narrator is stunned — Yozo saw himself as a monster, but others saw gentleness.

## Key Themes

1. **The Clown** — clowning as defense mechanism, the performer who hides behind humor
2. **Disqualified from Being Human** — the literal title; Yozo's conviction that he alone is not "human"
3. **Fear of Human Beings** — the terror that drives every relationship and performance
4. **Insincerity and Performance** — everyone performs; Yozo is the only one who knows he's performing
5. **The Weak Fear Happiness** — "The weak fear happiness itself. They can harm themselves on cotton wool."
6. **Love Suicide (心中, Shinjuu)** — the Japanese cultural phenomenon of joint suicide
7. **Morphine and Addiction** — escape from reality through substances
8. **Everything Passes** — Yozo's final, exhausted "truth"
