# Tsuneko

The café waitress with whom Yozo spends one night of genuine happiness — and who dies in the suicide pact at Kamakura.

---

## Profile

- **Occupation**: Café waitress in Ginza
- **Age**: Two years older than Yozo
- **Background**: From Hiroshima. Married to a barber who is in prison for swindling.
- **Personality**: Quiet, sad, worn down by poverty. "A silent current of misery an inch wide flowed over the surface of her body."

---

## The Night Together

Yozo goes to the café alone, broke. Tsuneko calms him without effort. He does not need to perform. He lies on the floor in his habitual gloom and she likes seeing him that way.

> "I had freed myself from fear and uneasiness."

This is the only time in the novel Yozo uses the word "happiness" without qualification. The night is "a night of liberation and happiness."

---

## The Morning After

In the morning, Yozo flees. "The weak fear happiness itself." He avoids her for a month. When he returns, she is even more worn. They attempt suicide together. She dies. He is saved.

Of all the people he had known, "that miserable Tsuneko really was the only one I loved."
