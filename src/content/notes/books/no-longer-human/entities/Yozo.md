# Yozo

The unnamed narrator and protagonist of *No Longer Human*. His name is never given in the notebooks — only the narrator and the café madam call him "Yozo."

---

## Profile

- **Background**: Wealthy family in rural Northeast Japan. Youngest child. Surrounded by women.
- **Appearance**: Described as extraordinarily handsome in student days. By 27, looks over 40.
- **Occupation**: College student (expelled), underground operative, second-rate cartoonist
- **Defining trait**: Conviction that he is "disqualified from being human"
- **Defense mechanism**: Clowning — humor as survival
- **Addiction**: Morphine
- **Marital status**: Married to Yoshiko (a woman of "divine ignorance")
- **Fate**: Institutionalized in a mental hospital, then exiled to the countryside. Possibly dead.

## The Paradox

Yozo sees himself as a monster. The café madam says: "He was a good boy, an angel." He cannot see his own gentleness because the clown has buried it so deeply.

In [[Crime and Punishment]], Raskolnikov's crime is an attempt to prove he's above humanity. Yozo's withdrawal is an attempt to escape humanity altogether. Both are wrong. Both are punished not by the law but by themselves.
