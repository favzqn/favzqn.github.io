# Horiki

Yozo's drinking companion and self-described "evil companion." A fellow student who introduces Yozo to bars, cafés, and the underground movement.

---

## Role

Horiki is the enabler. He drags Yozo to bars when he has no money. He introduces him to the café where Tsuneko works. He brings him into the Communist movement. He is present at every stage of Yozo's descent.

Horiki is not malicious. He is simply selfish and oblivious — a man who uses Yozo for entertainment and disappears when things get serious.

---

## The Kiss

At the café, Horiki insists on kissing the hostess. When he sees Tsuneko — poverty-stricken, worn — he recoils: "Not even a lecher like myself can kiss a woman who looks so poverty-stricken." This moment devastates Yozo, not because he loves Tsuneko, but because he sees in her poverty a mirror of his own.

---

## The Final Scene

Horiki appears at the mental hospital with Flatfish. He shows Yozo "a gentle smile, the like of which I had never before seen on his face." Yozo weeps. "I was completely shattered and smothered by that one gentle smile."

This is Horiki's cruelest act — not malice but accidental kindness. A single moment of gentleness destroys Yozo's last defense.
