# Insincerity and Performance

Yozo's observation is devastating: **everyone is performing**. He is the only one who knows it.

> "I am convinced that human life is filled with many pure, happy, serene examples of insincerity, truly splendid of their kind — of people deceiving one another without (strangely enough) any wounds being inflicted, of people who seem unaware even that they are deceiving one another."

---

## The Evidence

- At a political rally, the audience applauds enthusiastically, then walks home scathingly commenting on how boring it was. They arrive at Yozo's father's house and tell him with "genuine delight" how great it was.
- Servants complain bitterly about the meeting, then tell his mother it was "really interesting."
- His father asks what present he wants, then is disappointed when Yozo doesn't answer — but is delighted when he sneaks in and writes "lion mask" in the notebook.
- Flatfish never says what he means. He speaks in riddles, "intolerably prudent, circumlocutious" — and the simple fact that Yozo's family will fund his education is never stated.

---

## Yozo's Difference

Yozo performs too. But he **knows** he's performing. The others don't. Or worse — they do know, but they've normalized it. Insincerity is not a flaw in human society; it is the operating system.

Yozo's tragedy is that he takes sincerity seriously. He cannot lie casually — only desperately. He cannot perform effortlessly — only at "the cost of excruciating efforts within."
