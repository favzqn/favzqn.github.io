# Everything Passes

The novel's final "truth":

> "Now I have neither happiness nor unhappiness. Everything passes. That is the one and only thing I have thought resembled a truth in the society of human beings where I have dwelled up to now as in a burning hell."

---

## What It Is

Not wisdom. Exhaustion. Yozo has no energy left for happiness or unhappiness. He has been emptied. The line between acceptance and defeat has dissolved.

---

## The Ambiguity

Is "everything passes" a profound insight or a broken man's surrender? Dazai leaves it ambiguous. The phrase resonates because it is both:

- **As wisdom**: impermanence is the fundamental nature of experience. Happiness comes and goes. Pain comes and goes. The clown comes and goes.
- **As defeat**: Yozo has been so wounded that he can no longer feel. Numbness is not peace.

## Connection to Other Concepts

- [[Tao Te Ching]]: Lao Tzu teaches impermanence as a path to freedom. Yozo's impermanence is a path to nothing.
- [[The Uncarved Block]]: The uncarved block is the state before desire carves it. Yozo arrives at emptiness — but by destruction, not by nature.
- [[Desire Is Suffering]] (Naval): Naval says reduce desire → happiness. Yozo has reduced desire to zero → not happiness but blankness.
- [[The Weak Fear Happiness]]: "Everything passes" is the logical conclusion of fearing happiness — if everything passes, why attach to anything?
- [[Flow]]: Csikszentmihalyi says flow requires challenge-skill balance. Yozo has no challenge left. No flow. Only passing.
- [[The Old Man and the Sea]]: Santiago says "A man can be destroyed but not defeated." Yozo is the opposite — destroyed AND defeated.
