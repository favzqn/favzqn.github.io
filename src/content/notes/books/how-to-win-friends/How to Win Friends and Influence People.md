# How to Win Friends and Influence People

*How to Win Friends and Influence People* by Dale Carnegie (1936). The foundational text on interpersonal skills, communication, and leadership.

---

## Structure

### Part I: Fundamental Techniques in Handling People
- [[Don't Criticize, Condemn, or Complain]] — criticism is futile
- [[Give Honest and Sincere Appreciation]] — people crave appreciation
- [[Arouse in the Other Person an Eager Want]] — think from their perspective

### Part II: Six Ways to Make People Like You
- [[Become Genuinely Interested in Other People]] — interest begets interest
- Smile — a simple act with profound effects
- [[Remember That a Person's Name]] — the sweetest sound
- Be a Good Listener — encourage others to talk about themselves
- Talk in Terms of the Other Person's Interests — meet them where they are
- Make the Other Person Feel Important — and do it sincerely

### Part III: How to Win People to Your Way of Thinking
- [[Avoid Arguments]] — the only way to win is to avoid
- [[Show Respect for the Other Person's Opinion]] — never say "you're wrong"
- If You're Wrong, Admit It — admit quickly and emphatically
- Begin in a Friendly Way — honey catches more flies than vinegar
- Get the Other Person Saying Yes Immediately — start with agreement
- Let the Other Person Do Most of the Talking — listen
- [[Let the Other Person Feel the Idea Is Theirs]] — people love their own ideas
- Try Honestly to See Things from the Other Person's Point of View — empathy

### Part IV: Be a Leader — How to Change People Without Giving Offense
- Begin with Praise and Appreciation — start with the positive
- Call Attention to Mistakes Indirectly — hint at errors, don't state them
- Talk about Your Own Mistakes First — humility disarms
- Ask Questions Instead of Giving Orders — ask, don't tell
- Let the Other Person Save Face — protect their dignity
- Praise Every Improvement — small wins matter
- Give the Other Person a Fine Reputation to Live Up To — expectations shape behavior
- Make the Fault Seem Easy to Correct — make change feel achievable
- Make the Other Person Happy About Doing the Thing You Suggest — align with their desires
