# Abraham Lincoln

16th President of the United States (1809–1865). Led the nation through the Civil War and abolished slavery. A master of empathy and communication, he embodied many of Carnegie's principles.

---

## Key Qualities

- **Empathy**: Lincoln could see issues from the other person's perspective
- **Patience**: He was slow to anger and quick to forgive
- **Humility**: He admitted mistakes and learned from them
- **Kindness**: He was known for his gentleness and compassion
- **Humor**: He used humor to defuse tension and build rapport

---

## How Lincoln Embodied Carnegie's Principles

- He rarely criticized — he understood that criticism was futile
- He showed appreciation — his letters of praise are legendary
- He avoided arguments — he sought common ground
- He admitted when he was wrong — humility disarmed opponents
- He made others feel important — he listened and remembered names

---

## Famous Examples

- The letter he wrote to General Meade but never sent — criticizing indirectly
- His kindness to Copper Davis, who had insulted him
- His ability to lead a divided nation through empathy
- His Second Inaugural Address: "With malice toward none, with charity for all"
