# Never Split the Difference

*Never Split the Difference: Negotiating As If Your Life Depended On It* by Chris Voss and Tahl Raz (2016). A former FBI hostage negotiator teaches practical negotiation tactics based on real-world experience.

---

## Structure

### Chapter 1: The New Rules
- Negotiation is not about logic — it is about emotions
- The old rules of rational negotiation are wrong
- Hostage negotiation tactics apply to everyday life

### Chapter 2: Be a Mirror
- [[Mirroring]] — repeating the last 1–3 words they said
- [[Labeling]] — naming the other person's emotions
- Effective Pause — silence as a tool

### Chapter 3: Don't Feel Their Pain, Label It
- [[Labeling]] — "It seems like..." or "It sounds like..."
- Negative Labeling — acknowledging fears and concerns
- [[Calibrated Questions]] — "How" and "What" questions

### Chapter 4: Beware "Yes" — Master "No"
- [[The Power of No]] — people need to say no to feel safe
- [[The Accusation Audit]] — naming every negative thing up front
- The Clarifying No — "No" means "I'm not ready to agree"

### Chapter 5: Trigger "That's Right"
- [[That's Right]] — the magic phrase that signals breakthrough
- Summary — a paraphrase that gets "That's right"
- The No-Oriented Question — questions that lead to "No"

### Chapter 6: Bend Their Reality
- [[Anchoring]] — setting the first number
- The Ackerman Model — four-step anchoring process
- Loss Aversion — people fear loss more than they desire gain

### Chapter 7: Create the Illusion of Control
- [[Calibrated Questions]] — "How am I supposed to do that?"
- The 7-38-55 Rule — words, tone, body language
- [[Late-Night FM DJ Voice]] — calm, slow, downward-inflecting

### Chapter 8: Use Black Swans
- [[Black Swans]] — unknown unknowns that change everything
- [[The Accusation Audit]] — uncovering hidden fears
- Finding Black Swans — discovering the other side's deepest needs
