# The Power of No

In negotiations, "No" is not the end — it's the beginning. People need to say no to feel safe and in control. The goal is not to get "Yes" — it's to get to "That's right."

---

## Why "No" Is Powerful

- "Yes" is a commitment — people fear being trapped
- "No" is protection — it gives the other side a sense of safety
- People who say "No" feel in control
- "No" doesn't mean they disagree — it means they need to think
- "Yes" is often a counterfeit — people say yes to end the conversation

---

## The No-Oriented Question

- "Would it be ridiculous if we tried this?" → "No, it wouldn't be ridiculous"
- "Are you against starting today?" → "No, I'm not against it"
- "Is now a bad time to talk?" → "No, now is fine"
- Questions that lead to "No" give the other side a sense of control

---

## The Accusation Audit

- Name every negative thing the other side could say about you
- "You're probably thinking I'm being unreasonable..."
- "You might feel like I'm trying to take advantage..."
- By naming the negatives, you defuse them
- The other side thinks: "They get it"

---

## How to Practice

- **Use no-oriented questions** — "Would it be ridiculous if...?"
- **Don't push for "Yes"** — it's a counterfeit anyway
- **Name the negatives** — the accusation audit
- **Let them say no** — it builds trust and control
- **Aim for "That's right"** — not "Yes"
