# The Intelligent Investor

*The Intelligent Investor: A Book of Practical Counsel* by Benjamin Graham, updated with commentary by Jason Zweig (4th edition, 2003). Warren Buffett calls it "by far the best book on investing ever written."

---

## Summary

Graham's foundational text on value investing. The book does not teach how to beat the market — it teaches how to minimize losses, maximize sustainable gains, and control self-defeating behavior. Jason Zweig's commentary updates Graham's 1973 text with modern examples while preserving every word of the original.

### The Two Investors

Graham divides all investors into two categories:

**The Defensive Investor**: Seeks safety and freedom from bother. Should maintain a 50/50 split between high-grade bonds and leading common stocks, rebalancing when allocations drift by 5%. Should buy diversified lists of stocks meeting specific criteria (adequate size, strong financial condition, dividends for 20+ years, no earnings deficit in 10 years).

**The Enterprising Investor**: Willing to devote time and effort to selecting securities. Can pursue net-current-asset bargains, special situations, and bargain issues. Must have the temperament for patience and the analytical skill for security analysis.

### Mr. Market

The book's most famous allegory: Mr. Market is a manic-depressive business partner who appears every day offering to buy your shares or sell you his. Some days he's euphoric (high prices); other days he's depressed (low prices). He is there to serve you, not to instruct you. You are free to ignore him entirely.

### The Margin of Safety

The central concept: always buy securities at a significant discount to their intrinsic value. This cushion protects against errors in analysis, bad luck, and market volatility. "The margin of safety is always dependent on the price paid."

### Key Chapters

1. **Investment vs Speculation**: Investment promises safety and adequate return. Everything else is speculation.
2. **The Investor and Inflation**: Stocks are a decent hedge against moderate inflation, but not against severe inflation. REITs and TIPS provide additional protection.
3. **A Century of Stock-Market History**: Market cycles repeat. Don't extrapolate recent returns into the future.
4. **General Portfolio Policy**: The 50/50 split. Dollar-cost averaging.
5. **The Defensive Investor and Common Stocks**: Seven statistical requirements for stock selection.
6. **Portfolio Policy — Negative Approach**: What to avoid (popular stocks, IPOs, hot tips).
7. **Portfolio Policy — Positive Approach**: Net-current-asset bargains, special situations.
8. **The Investor and Market Fluctuations**: Market fluctuations create opportunity, not danger, for the intelligent investor.
9. **Investing in Investment Funds**: Most funds fail to beat the market after fees.
10. **The Investor and His Advisers**: You must ultimately make your own decisions.
11. **Security Analysis for the Lay Investor**: P/E ratios, earnings stability, dividend records, financial position.
12. **Per-Share Earnings**: How to interpret earnings data skeptically.
13-13. **Case Histories and Comparisons**: Four companies, eight pairs analyzed.
14-15. **Stock Selection**: Specific criteria for defensive and enterprising investors.
16. **Convertible Issues and Warrants**: High-risk instruments.
17. **Four Extremely Instructive Case Histories**: TWA, Bunker R., Commonwealth Trust, Penn Central.
18. **Shareholders and Management**: Dividend policy, corporate governance.
19. **"Margin of Safety" as the Central Concept of Investment**: The capstone chapter.

### Appendix: The Superinvestors of Graham-and-Doddsville

Warren Buffett's famous 1984 speech demonstrating that a group of investors trained by Graham consistently beat the market — disproving the efficient-market hypothesis.

## Key Themes

1. **Margin of Safety** — buy below intrinsic value
2. **Mr. Market** — use the market, don't let it use you
3. **Defensive vs Enterprising** — know your category
4. **Market Fluctuations** — opportunity, not danger
5. **Inflation Protection** — stocks + bonds + REITs + TIPS
6. **Self-Discipline** — the investor's worst enemy is himself
7. **Simplicity** — resist unnecessary complexity
8. **Temperament Over Intelligence** — emotional control matters more than IQ
