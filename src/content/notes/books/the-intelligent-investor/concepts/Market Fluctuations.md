# Market Fluctuations

"The true investor will rarely if ever be forced to sell his shares. He can be patient and ignore the market's swings."

---

## The Paradox

Stock prices fluctuate constantly, but the underlying businesses change slowly. The intelligent investor views price fluctuations as **opportunity**, not danger.

When prices fall, the margin of safety increases. When prices rise, it decreases. The rational response is to buy more when prices are low and buy less (or nothing) when prices are high.

---

## The Psychological Trap

Most investors do the opposite. They feel confident when prices are high (because their portfolio is up) and terrified when prices are low (because it's down). This is the "rule of opposites" — by the time most investors are confident enough to buy, prices are already expensive.

---

## Graham's Data

From Graham's analysis of the S&P 500:
- When P/E ratios are below 10, subsequent 10-year returns average 10%+
- When P/E ratios are above 20, subsequent 10-year returns average 0-5%
- The market has never stayed at either extreme for long
