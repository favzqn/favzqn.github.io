# Margin of Safety

The central concept of investment, according to Benjamin Graham.

"The margin of safety is always dependent on the price paid. It will be large at one price, small at some higher price, nonexistent at some still higher price."

---

## The Principle

Every investment carries uncertainty. You cannot eliminate risk, but you can create a cushion against it. The margin of safety is the gap between what you pay and what the investment is worth. If a stock is worth $100 and you buy it at $60, your margin of safety is $40 — you can be $40 wrong and still not lose money.

---

## Why It Works

Graham identifies three sources of investment risk:

1. **Risk from business or financial changes** — the company itself deteriorates
2. **Risk from general market declines** — the whole market falls
3. **Risk from individual overpayment** — you paid too much

The margin of safety directly addresses the third risk, and partially mitigates the first two. Even a good company becomes a bad investment if you overpay.

---

## The Psychology

Graham observed that investors consistently overpay during bull markets and refuse to buy during bear markets — exactly backwards. The margin of safety provides a rational framework that overrides emotional decision-making.

Jason Zweig's commentary: "The margin of safety runs on a spectrum from thin to thick — the fatter the cushion, the less the investor has to get everything right."
