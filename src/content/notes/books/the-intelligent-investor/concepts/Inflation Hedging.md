# Inflation Hedging

"The investor cannot afford to put all his funds into one basket — neither in the bond basket nor in the stock basket."

---

## The Evidence

Graham shows that stocks are a **decent** hedge against moderate inflation, but not a **perfect** one:
- In years of moderate inflation (2-4%), stocks generally do well
- In years of high inflation (6%+), stocks lose money 57% of the time
- In years of deflation, stocks also lose money

The relationship is not linear. Mild inflation is good for stocks; extreme inflation (or deflation) is bad.

---

## The Solution

Graham and Zweig recommend a diversified approach:
1. **Stocks** — hedge against moderate inflation
2. **Bonds** — provide income and stability
3. **REITs** — real estate investment trusts hedge against real-asset inflation
4. **TIPS** — Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities guarantee inflation-adjusted returns

The 50/50 split between stocks and bonds provides natural inflation protection without requiring the investor to predict inflation.
