# Investment vs Speculation

Graham's foundational distinction:

**Investment**: "An operation which upon thorough analysis promises safety of principal and an adequate return. Operations not meeting these requirements are speculative."

---

## The Three Criteria

1. **Thorough analysis** — you've studied the fundamentals
2. **Safety of principal** — you're unlikely to lose your money
3. **Adequate return** — the expected return justifies the risk

If any one of these is missing, you're speculating, not investing.

---

## The Spectrum

Graham is not anti-speculation. He recognizes that speculation has its place. The danger is **disguised speculation** — when people think they're investing but are actually gambling. This happens when:

- You buy a stock because it's "going up" (not because it's undervalued)
- You follow tips from friends or media
- You put more than 10% of your portfolio in speculative positions
- You confuse optimism with analysis

---

## The Modern Problem

Zweig's commentary notes that "in 2003, the line between investing and speculation is as blurred as it has ever been." Day trading, IPO flipping, and momentum strategies are all disguised as investing.
