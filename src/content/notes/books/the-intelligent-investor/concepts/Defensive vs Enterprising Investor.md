# Defensive vs Enterprising Investor

Graham divides all investors into two categories:

---

## The Defensive Investor

- Seeks **safety** and **freedom from bother**
- Maintains a 50/50 split between high-grade bonds and leading common stocks
- Rebalances when allocations drift by ~5%
- Uses dollar-cost averaging
- Selects stocks meeting 7 criteria: adequate size, strong financial condition, dividends for 20+ years, no earnings deficit in 10 years, 33% earnings growth over 10 years, P/E under 15, price below 1.5x book value

The defensive investor's principal problem is **simplicity**. They are tempted by complexity because it feels more sophisticated.

---

## The Enterprising Investor

- Willing to devote **time and effort** to selecting securities
- Can pursue net-current-asset bargains (stocks trading below net working capital)
- Can pursue special situations (mergers, liquidations, reorganizations)
- Can buy bargain issues of secondary companies
- Must have the **temperament** for patience and the **analytical skill** for security analysis

The enterprising investor's principal problem is **discipline**. They are tempted to over-trade and chase performance.

---

## The Key Insight

Most people who think they are enterprising investors are really defensive investors who have deluded themselves. Graham's advice: "Confronted with a challenge to follow simplicity, most investors succumb to unnecessary complexity."
