# Persuasion Tactics

*Persuasion Tactics: Covert Psychology Strategies to Influence, Persuade, Get Your Way (Without Manipulation)* by Patrick King (2017).

---

## Summary

A comprehensive guide to persuasion psychology, organized from overt tactics (direct arguments) to covert tactics (invisible influence). King covers 18 chapters spanning communication styles, emotional debt, framing, subconscious linguistics, and even a case study of Hitler's persuasion methods.

### Part 1: Foundation

**Ch 1 — The Butterfly Effect**: Persuasion is how you get anything you want in life. Small changes in approach produce massive changes in outcomes.

**Ch 2 — Understand Your Actual Audience**: The people who can be persuaded are those on the fence. Don't waste energy on those who can't be moved.

**Ch 3 — Creating Emotional Debt**: Reciprocity and obligation — when someone feels they owe you, they act against their own interests.

**Ch 4 — Communication Styles**: Four types — Analytical (data-driven), Intuitive (big picture), Personal (relationship-focused), Functional (process-oriented). Match their style to be heard.

### Part 2: Overt Persuasion

**Ch 5 — Likability As Lubricant**: Being charming makes people go along with you because they like you and want to reciprocate positive feelings.

**Ch 6 — Manufacturing External Credibility**: Ways to appear supported and validated even when you objectively lack credentials.

**Ch 7 — Elements of Persuasive Presentation**: Perceived accuracy, simplicity, completeness, playing devil's advocate to appear unbiased.

**Ch 8 — Exact Persuasive Phrases**: Speak in absolutes, allude to others, control the frame, use "ultimate terms."

**Ch 9 — The Classic Challenge**: Reverse psychology — using people's pride, ego, and autonomy against them.

**Ch 10 — Constructing a Persuasive Frame**: Characterize options to maximize your pros and minimize your cons, and vice versa for the opposing option.

### Part 3: Psychological Tactics

**Ch 11 — Capitalize on Human Selfishness**: "What's in it for me?" — frame choices in terms of their benefits.

**Ch 12 — Utilize Maslow's Hierarchy**: Understand where people fall on the needs spectrum and tailor your argument accordingly.

**Ch 13 — Appeal to Ethos, Pathos, Logos**: Aristotle's framework — credibility, emotion, and logic. All three must be present.

**Ch 14 — Subconscious Linguistics**: Phrasing that makes commands and suggestions subconscious.

**Ch 15 — Mental Strategies**: Promise of reward, promise of punishment, confusion/disrupt-and-reframe, debt, moral appeal, positive/negative self-feeling, altercasting, altruism, exclusivity, leading to the light, foot-in-the-door, door-in-the-face.

### Part 4: Covert and Defensive

**Ch 16 — Underhanded Persuasion**: Double bind, praeteritio, paralipsis, straw man — tactics to recognize and defend against.

**Ch 17 — Planting Ideas**: Embrace/accept the status quo, talk around the idea, play dumb — make people think they generated the idea.

**Ch 18 — Hitler's Persuasion**: False dichotomy, in-groups, amplifying negative emotions, providing change as the solution.

---

## Key Themes

1. **Emotion Over Logic** — people decide on emotion, then justify with logic
2. **Covert > Overt** — the best persuasion is invisible
3. **Frame Control** — whoever defines the options controls the outcome
4. **Know Your Audience** — different people need different approaches
5. **Self-Image Is the Lever** — appeal to how people see themselves
6. **Plant, Don't Push** — ideas people generate themselves are irresistible
7. **Defense Is Essential** — understanding persuasion protects you from manipulation
