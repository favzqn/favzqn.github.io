# Planting Ideas

"When someone feels like they have generated an idea by themselves, they have no barriers to acting upon it."

---

## The Four Methods

### 1. Embrace the Status Quo
Be happy about NOT having what you want them to buy. Drop benefits casually while appearing content without it.

"No, I love walking. Sure, it would be great to have a heater in winter, but hey! It builds character."

### 2. Accept the Status Quo
Bemoan the barriers to change. Show displeasure with the current situation. Make the status quo feel unbearable.

"We don't need a car. I guess we'll just deal with the negative consequences of taking the bus everywhere."

### 3. Talk Around the Idea
Discuss adjacent topics — transportation, commuting, costs — without directly suggesting the idea. Inundate them until they arrive at your conclusion.

### 4. Play Dumb
Ask innocent questions that let the other person brainstorm your solution.

"How am I supposed to get to work faster and not have to walk as much? I'm just so lost!"

---

## Why It Works

People resist persuasion because it threatens autonomy. When an idea feels self-generated, resistance vanishes. The target's ego actually strengthens the idea — they want to prove how clever they are.
